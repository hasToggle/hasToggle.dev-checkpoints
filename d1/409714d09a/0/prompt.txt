## 1. Knowledge conflict taxonomy

**"Knowledge Conflicts for LLMs: A Survey"** — Rongwu Xu, Zehan Qi, Zhijiang Guo, Cunxiang Wang, Hongru Wang, Yue Zhang, Wei Xu. arXiv:2403.08319 (v1 2024-03-13, v2 2024-06-22). Repo: https://github.com/pillowsofwind/Knowledge-Conflicts-Survey **[V]** https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08319 · https://arxiv.org/html/2403.08319v2

Three-way taxonomy **[V]**:

| Type | Definition | Causes | Relevance to your system |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Context-Memory (CM)** | Retrieved/prompt context vs. the LLM's parametric knowledge | Temporal misalignment; misinformation pollution | Your extractor's priors fighting the meeting transcript |
| **Inter-Context (IC)** | Contradiction *among* pieces of context (retrieved docs, dialogue history) | Misinformation; outdated info mixed with fresh in the same corpus | **This is your core problem**: two meeting notes disagreeing about a person's role |
| **Intra-Memory (IM)** | Model gives divergent answers to semantically equivalent inputs | Training corpus bias; stochastic decoding; knowledge editing that doesn't generalize | Your extractor emitting different facts from paraphrases of the same utterance |

Solution families the survey catalogs **[V]**: for CM — *faithful-to-context* (KAFT, CAD decoding, OPIN prompting, ICLM), *discriminating misinformation* (defense prompting, query augmentation/CAR, discriminator training), *disentangling sources* (DisentQA — generate separate context-answer and memory-answer), *improving factuality* (COMBO, CD²). For IC — *eliminating conflict* (PCNN, LLM fact-checking with external tools) and *improving robustness* (discriminator+decoder fine-tuning, confidence-scored query augmentation). For IM — consistency loss fine-tuning, ConCoRD output ensembling, DoLa contrastive decoding, ITI.

Reported behavioral findings (relayed by extraction from the HTML — spot-check before quoting) **[V, extraction-relayed]**: LLMs show significant bias toward evidence aligned with parametric memory ("confirmation bias"); contradiction-detection accuracy is characterized as "subpar"; BERT/RoBERTa ~50–60% knowledge-consistency accuracy; GPT-4 ~13% inconsistency on commonsense; ~40% gap between knowledge-probe accuracy and generation accuracy. The survey's own conclusion: **no one-size-fits-all solution**; the mitigation objectives literally conflict (be faithful to context vs. protect parametric knowledge).

**Design takeaway.** Your review queue is essentially a human-in-the-loop *inter-context conflict* resolver. The survey says machines are bad at this; that justifies the queue.

## 2. Contradiction detection with NLI — what actually works, and the honest numbers

### 2.1 Off-the-shelf models

**MoritzLaurer/DeBERTa-v3-large-mnli-fever-anli-ling-wanli** **[V]** https://huggingface.co/MoritzLaurer/DeBERTa-v3-large-mnli-fever-anli-ling-wanli
- 0.4B params, trained on 885,242 NLI premise/hypothesis pairs (MNLI + Fever-NLI + ANLI + LingNLI + WANLI).
- **Label order: `[entailment, neutral, contradiction]`** — index 2 is contradiction. (Different NLI checkpoints use different orderings; always read `model.config.id2label`.)
- MNLI-m 0.912, MNLI-mm 0.908, ANLI-all 0.702, ANLI-r3 0.64, WANLI 0.77, LingNLI 0.87.

**vectara/hallucination_evaluation_model (HHEM-2.1-Open)** **[V]** https://huggingface.co/vectara/hallucination_evaluation_model — flan-t5-base, unlimited context, outputs a 0–1 *factual-consistency* score (`model.predict([(premise, hypothesis)])`). AggreFact-SOTA balanced acc 76.55% / F1 66.77; RAGTruth-Summ 64.42 / 44.83; RAGTruth-QA 74.28 / 60.00. Cheap enough to run on every candidate pair.

Common practical thresholding **[V, from a survey writeup]** https://mbrenndoerfer.com/writing/hallucination-detection — flag when P(contradiction) &gt; 0.9; a two-checkpoint ensemble requiring both to exceed 0.7 is a common precision-boosting trick.

### 2.2 The hard truths (read these before you build)

**de Marneffe, Rafferty &amp; Manning (ACL 2008), "Finding Contradictions in Text"** **[V]** https://aclanthology.org/P08-1118.pdf

Their **contradiction typology** — use this directly as your feature/rule taxonomy:

| # | Type | Detectability |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Antonym** ("catalyst for crime" vs "deterrent to crime") | Easy — closed-set lexical resources |
| 2 | **Negation** | Easy |
| 3 | **Numeric/date mismatch** ("killed 50" vs "28 confirmed dead") | Easy |
| 4–5 | **Factive/modal** ("says he will not withdraw" vs "withdraws") | Hard |
| 6–7 | **Structural** (argument-role swap: "Santer succeeded Delors" vs "Delors succeeded Santer") | Hard |
| 8–9 | **Lexical** ("said X did nothing wrong" vs "accuses X") | Hard |
| 10 | **World knowledge** ("Microsoft Israel founded 1989" vs "Microsoft established 1989") | Hard |

Their measured performance **[V]** (Table 5): RTE1-dev1 P 70.37 / R 40.43; RTE2-dev P 64.00 / R 28.83; RTE3-dev P 61.90 / R 31.71; **RTE1-test P 42.22 / R 26.21; RTE3-test P 22.95 / R 19.44**. Precision collapses out-of-distribution. Filtering strategy mattered a lot: "no filter" P 55.10 / R 32.93 → "root + topic alignment filter" P 61.90 / R 31.71.

**The single most important insight in that paper** **[V]**: a mismatch is only a contradiction if the two statements are *about the same event/entity*. Their example: "Three Iraqi soldiers wounded near Beiji" vs "Three Iraqi soldiers died near Adhaim" is **not** a contradiction (different events); "Kennedy assassinated in Texas" vs "Kennedy's murder occurred in Washington" **is** (unique event). → **Never run NLI on an unkeyed pair. Key on (subject, predicate) first, then run NLI on the residual.** This is exactly what Graphiti/TOKI/Engram all do.

**DECODE (Nie, Williamson, Bansal, Kiela, Weston), arXiv 2012.13391** **[V]** https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13391 — NLI training does *not* transfer to conversational contradiction:

| Approach | Training data | Main (Test) | Human-Bot (OOD) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unstructured RoBERTa | DNLI only | 57.19 | 60.34 |
| Unstructured RoBERTa | ANLI-R3 only | 82.21 | 59.69 |
| Unstructured RoBERTa | DECODE | 96.85 | 70.03 |
| **Utterance-based** RoBERTa | SNLI+MNLI | 77.40 | 73.17 |
| **Utterance-based** RoBERTa | DECODE | 93.19 | **84.69** |
| **Utterance-based** RoBERTa | All | 94.19 | 83.64 |
| Majority baseline | — | 50.00 | 50.00 |

Two lessons: (a) the **structured, utterance-pair** formulation (compare candidate against each prior utterance separately) is far more OOD-robust than shoving the whole history in as one premise; (b) generic MNLI-only transfers weakly (73.17 OOD).

**Decontextualization is a prerequisite.** "Improving Bot Response Contradiction Detection via Utterance Rewriting," arXiv 2207.11862 **[V, abstract]** https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.11862 — utterances contain coreference and ellipsis; rewriting them into self-contained form before NLI measurably improves contradiction detection. **This validates your "atomic facts" design**: store facts as self-contained propositions (Zep does exactly this: "Each fact represents a self-contained proposition that includes entity names and constitutes an atomic unit of context-free information" **[V]**).

Also: **"A Straightforward Pipeline for Targeted Entailment and Contradiction Detection," arXiv 2508.17127** **[V]** https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.17127 — aggregate token-level attention to shortlist contextually-salient candidate sentences, *then* run pretrained NLI on each candidate, then filter NLI verdicts by attention saliency. Same two-stage shape: cheap retrieval gate → expensive semantic judge.

## 3. Belief revision for agent memory

### 3.1 AGM — the postulates you'd actually be implementing

Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Logic of Belief Revision" **[V]** https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/logic-belief-revision/

Three operations: **expansion** `K+p = Cn(K ∪ {p})`, **contraction** `K÷p`, **revision** `K*p`.

Basic revision postulates **[V]**:
1. **Closure**: `K*p = Cn(K*p)`
2. **Success**: `p ∈ K*p`
3. **Inclusion**: `K*p ⊆ K+p`
4. **Vacuity**: if `¬p ∉ K` then `K*p = K+p`
5. **Consistency**: `K*p` is consistent if `p` is
6. **Extensionality**: if `⊢ p↔q` then `K*p = K*q`
7. **Superexpansion**: `K*(p∧q) ⊆ (K*p)+q`
8. **Subexpansion**: if `¬q ∉ Cn(K*p)` then `(K*p)+q ⊆ K*(p∧q)`

Contraction postulates: Closure, Success, Inclusion, Vacuity, Extensionality, **Recovery** (`K ⊆ (K÷p)+p`), Conjunctive Inclusion, Conjunctive Overlap. **[V]**

- **Levi identity**: `K*p = (K÷¬p)+p` — *revision = contract by the negation, then expand.* **[V]**
- **Harper identity**: `K÷p = K ∩ K*¬p` **[R]**
- **Partial meet contraction**: `K÷p = ⋂γ(K⊥p)` where `K⊥p` is the set of inclusion-maximal subsets of `K` not implying `p`, and `γ` is a selection function picking the "best" remainders. **[V]**
- **Epistemic entrenchment** (`≤`) postulates: Transitivity, Dominance (`p⊢q ⇒ p≤q`), Conjunctiveness, Minimality, Maximality; and contraction via entrenchment: `q ∈ K÷p iff q ∈ K and either p &lt; (p∨q) or p ∈ Cn(∅)`. **[V]**

**Entrenchment is your practical hook.** In an engineering system, "epistemic entrenchment" = the priority ordering you use to decide which fact survives: source tier &gt; explicit user correction &gt; confidence &gt; recency. Implement `≤` as a total preorder over facts and you get AGM-compatible minimal change for free.

### 3.2 Truth maintenance systems

Doyle's **TMS** (1979) and de Kleer's **ATMS** (1986) maintain a dependency network: each belief node records the *justifications* (antecedent node sets) that support it, and retraction propagates through the justification graph rather than requiring recomputation. ATMS labels each node with the set of minimal *environments* (assumption sets) under which it holds, plus a **nogood** set of inconsistent assumption combinations — letting multiple contexts coexist simultaneously instead of maintaining one consistent state. **[R, unverified — I did not fetch de Kleer's papers]**

Practical descendant, verified: **IMPACT-CYCLE (Kong et al., 2026)** — each claim carries a dependency graph; corrections propagate *only* to structurally dependent claims. **[V, via survey]** described in https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.30306

### 3.3 2026 papers applying belief revision to agent memory

**Kumiho — "Graph-Native Cognitive Memory for AI Agents: Formal Belief Revision Semantics"** (Young Bin Park, 2026) **[V, read the PDF]** https://kumiho.io/pdfs/kumiho_AI_cognitive_memory_paper.pdf

Neo4j property graph. Immutable **revisions** + mutable **tag pointers**. Conflicts resolved via a `Supersedes` edge that creates a new revision. Two-tier belief state:
- `B(τ)` = full operator-accessible store (all revisions incl. archived).
- `B_retr(τ)` = agent retrieval surface: revision `r` is retrievable **iff** (i) referenced by an active tag and (ii) its item is not `deprecated`.

Crucially: **both fulltext and vector retrieval branches carry a mandatory `WHERE NOT item.deprecated` clause in the Cypher**, so exclusion is architecturally enforced, not application-dependent. This is the fix for the failure mode where a superseded revision leaks back via embedding similarity and re-introduces the contradiction. **[V]**

Postulate satisfaction table **[V]** (Hansson belief-base framing):

| Postulate | Mechanism | Status |
|---|---|---|
| K*2 Success | New revision contains A; tag updated | ✓ |
| K*3 Inclusion | Base-level: `B*A ⊆ B ∪ {A}` | ✓ |
| K*4 Vacuity | No conflict ⇒ no retraction needed | ✓ |
| K*5 Consistency | Supersedes *replaces*, not accumulates | ✓ |
| K*6 Extensionality | Syntactic = logical equiv. for ground atoms | ✓ |
| Relevance (Hansson) | Tag removal targets relevant revisions | ✓ |
| Core-Retainment (Hansson) | Removed beliefs contributed to contracted belief | ✓ |
| K*7/K*8 Super/Sub-expansion | argued, not established | open |

They **explicitly reject Recovery** (`K ⊆ (K÷p)+p`), following Makinson (1987) and Hansson (1991), on the grounds that immutable versioning makes it both unnecessary and wrong — replaced by Relevance + Core-Retainment. **[V]**

Numbers **[V]**: 97.5% adversarial refusal accuracy (n=446); overall F1 0.565 (n=1,986) including adversarial binary scoring; automated AGM compliance testing across 49 scenarios × 7 postulates → 100% operational adherence; on LoCoMo-Plus, 93.3% judge accuracy (n=401) vs. best published baseline Gemini 2.5 Pro at 45.7% (+47.6 pts); recall accuracy 98.5% (395/401); GPT-4o-mini→GPT-4o swap moves end-to-end from ~88% → 93.3%; total cost ~$14 for 401 entries.

**"When Does Belief-Based Agent Memory Help? Reliability-Conditional Updating and Provenance-Capped Poisoning Defense"** (system "Nous"), arXiv 2606.22030 **[V, fetched HTML]** https://arxiv.org/html/2606.22030

This is the single most decision-relevant paper for you. Concrete mechanics:

- Beliefs are **categorical distributions over entity-attribute pairs**, not deterministic conclusions. Bayesian posterior:
  `p_post(v) = ℒ(obs|v)·p_prior(v) / Σ_v' ℒ(obs|v')·p_prior(v')`
  with `ℒ(obs|v) = 1−ε` if `v` matches the observation, `ε/(|V|−1)` otherwise; `ε = 0.01`.
- **Surprise** `S(obs|D) = −log₂ p(obs)` bits, drives which observations trigger real belief shifts.
- **Entropy decay / semantic forgetting**: `p_t(v) = λ^Δt·p_0(v) + (1−λ^Δt)·U(v)` — beliefs revert toward uniform, rather than time-based deletion.
- **Identity resolution**: merge entities whose symmetrised KL divergence over shared attributes is below a threshold, via weighted posterior mixture.
- **Multi-valued attributes**: at query time include *every* observed value with surprise &gt; θ_min, preserving historical alternatives alongside the top-confidence belief.

**The killer negative result [V]:** *Bayesian belief updating is worthless without a per-observation reliability signal.* On LoCoMo, replacing Bayesian updating with naive last-write-wins gave **identical** accuracy (token-F1 36.27 vs 36.77). Only when reliability signals were added did belief-updating pull ahead — on their controlled contradiction benchmark (60 scenarios): **Nous+reliability 100, LLM-append-retrieve 90, last-write-wins 67, frequency-based 46.**

**Provenance cap:** `r = min(provenance_tier, content_confidence)`. Provenance tier assigned by *channel* (oracle, not learned); content confidence extracted from epistemic markers (hedging "maybe/I think" → ~0.23 mean; definite "confirmed/definitely" → ~0.95 mean). **Content-inferred trust alone is adversarially gameable** — a confident-sounding false injection scored 0.96. Results: 50 poison observations vs 4 trusted ones on a 0.2-tier channel → **0% attack success** with provenance capping; **100% success** with content-only trust; **100%** with last-write-wins. Laundering through a trusted intermediary defeats naive tiering entirely — only taint-tracking restores 0% (characterized but not implemented). **[V]**

Also flagged: metric fragility — on identical outputs, strict token-F1 averaged 32.45 vs LLM-judge 59.97, a **27.5-point macro gap**. **[V]**

Related: "On Definite Iterated Belief Revision with Belief Algebras," arXiv 2505.06505 **[V, citation only]** https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06505

## 4. Temporal KG fact invalidation — bitemporal modeling and how Zep/Graphiti actually does it

### 4.1 Bitemporal basics

Two orthogonal axes (Snodgrass; SQL:2011 system-versioned + application-time tables) **[V, as cited in TOKI]**:
- **Valid time** `[valid_from, valid_to)` — when the fact was/is true *in the world*.
- **Transaction time** `[system_time_start, system_time_end)` — when the *system* believed it.

Invariant everyone converges on: **never hard-delete; close the interval.** "As-of" retrieval = filter on the four timestamp columns.

### 4.2 Zep paper (arXiv 2501.13956) — verbatim mechanics

**[V — I extracted the PDF text directly]** https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.13956

&gt; "Zep implements a bi-temporal model, where timeline **T** represents the chronological ordering of events, and timeline **T′** represents the transactional order of Zep's data ingestion."

&gt; "the system tracks four timestamps: **t′_created** and **t′_expired ∈ T′** monitor when facts are created or invalidated in the system, while **t_valid** and **t_invalid ∈ T** track the temporal range during which facts held true. These temporal data points are stored on edges alongside other fact information."

&gt; "The introduction of new edges can invalidate existing edges in the database. The system employs an LLM to compare new edges against semantically related existing edges to identify potential contradictions. **When the system identifies temporally overlapping contradictions, it invalidates the affected edges by setting their t_invalid to the t_valid of the invalidating edge.** Following the transactional timeline T′, Graphiti consistently prioritizes new information when determining edge invalidation."

Other operational details from the paper **[V]**: entity extraction uses the current message + last **n = 4** messages for NER context; a reflexion-style reflection pass reduces hallucination; entity names embedded into **1024-dim** vectors; cosine similarity + full-text search produce candidate nodes for LLM-based entity resolution; **edge dedup hybrid search is constrained to edges between the same entity pair** (prevents cross-pair mixing, shrinks the search space); community detection uses **label propagation** (not Leiden) so it extends dynamically.

`graphiti_core/utils/maintenance/edge_operations.py` — the candidate retrieval **[V]**:
- **Dedupe candidates**: `search(config=EDGE_HYBRID_SEARCH_RRF, search_filter=SearchFilters(edge_uuids=[...same node-pair edges...]))` — *constrained to the same entity pair*.

**The temporal guard — verbatim [V]:**

```python
def resolve_edge_contradictions(resolved_edge, invalidation_candidates) -&gt; list[EntityEdge]:
    invalidated_edges = []
    for edge in invalidation_candidates:
        # Skip if the intervals cannot possibly conflict
        if (edge.invalid_at is not None and resolved_edge.valid_at is not None
                and edge.invalid_at &lt;= resolved_edge.valid_at) \
           or (edge.valid_at is not None and resolved_edge.invalid_at is not None
                and resolved_edge.invalid_at &lt;= edge.valid_at):
            continue
        # Only invalidate a STRICTLY OLDER edge
        elif (edge.valid_at is not None and resolved_edge.valid_at is not None
                and edge.valid_at &lt; resolved_edge.valid_at):
            edge.invalid_at = resolved_edge.valid_at
            edge.expired_at = edge.expired_at or utc_now()
            invalidated_edges.append(edge)
    return invalidated_edges
```

**And the out-of-order-ingestion path — the incoming edge can be born already-dead [V]:**

```python
now = utc_now()
if resolved_edge.invalid_at and not resolved_edge.expired_at:
    resolved_edge.expired_at = now

if resolved_edge.expired_at is None:
    invalidation_candidates.sort(key=lambda c: (c.valid_at is None, ensure_utc(c.valid_at)))
    for candidate in invalidation_candidates:
        if candidate.valid_at &gt; resolved_edge.valid_at:
            resolved_edge.invalid_at = candidate.valid_at   # new fact is HISTORICAL
            resolved_edge.expired_at = now
            break
```

That's the backfill case: if you ingest an old email after a newer meeting, the old email's fact is inserted already-invalidated. Essential for a system that ingests email — email arrives out of chronological order constantly.

**Temporal extraction prompt rules** (`prompts/extract_edges.py`) **[V]**:
- `valid_at` / `invalid_at` as ISO 8601 with `Z`.
- "If the fact is ongoing (present tense), set `valid_at` to the timestamp of the episode the fact originates from. If no per-episode timestamp is available, use REFERENCE_TIME."
- "If a change/termination is expressed, set `invalid_at` to the relevant timestamp."
- "Do **not** hallucinate or infer temporal bounds from unrelated events."
- "If only a date is mentioned (no time), assume 00:00:00." / year only → Jan 1 00:00:00.
- System prompt: *"You extract temporal bounds from facts. NEVER hallucinate dates."*

Zep's docs describe an additional nicety **[V]** https://blog.getzep.com/beyond-static-knowledge-graphs/ — after invalidation, Graphiti **regenerates the fact string**: "Maria works as a junior manager" → "Maria used to work as a junior manager, until her promotion to a senior manager."

**Known failure mode [V]** https://github.com/getzep/graphiti/issues/1666 — with non-reasoning small models, the stock `EdgeDuplicate` response schema produces near-total failure on contradiction detection: temporal invalidation *silently* underperforms and stale facts survive their own contradiction. A reasoning-first response schema restores it. **If you use a small model for this step, measure it.**

### 4.4 Temporal KG completion literature

"A Survey on Temporal Knowledge Graph: Representation Learning and Applications," arXiv 2403.04782 **[V, abstract]** https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.04782 — comprehensive survey of TKG representation learning, with a taxonomy by core technology and a datasets/metrics section. The standard split (interpolation = completing facts *within* the observed time range, a.k.a. TKG completion; extrapolation = forecasting facts at *future* timestamps) is standard in this literature. **[R for the interpolation/extrapolation framing specifically — the abstract page didn't surface the taxonomy detail.]**

Practical note: this embedding-based literature is largely orthogonal to what you need. You need *invalidation bookkeeping*, not link prediction over ICEWS/GDELT.

## 5. "Update vs. coexist" — the actual decision, with concrete algorithms from five systems

### 5.1 Mem0 — ADD / UPDATE / DELETE / NONE

Paper: arXiv 2504.19413 **[V, HTML]** https://arxiv.org/html/2504.19413v1 · Code **[V, downloaded]** https://github.com/mem0ai/mem0/blob/main/mem0/configs/prompts.py

Mechanics **[V]**: two phases. Extraction φ over `(m_{t−1}, m_t)` plus a conversation summary S. Update phase retrieves the **top s = 10 semantically similar memories** by vector similarity, then a **tool-call** forces one of the four ops.

The actual production prompt (`DEFAULT_UPDATE_MEMORY_PROMPT`), verbatim rules **[V]**:

&gt; **1. Add**: "If the retrieved facts contain new information not present in the memory, then you have to add it by generating a new ID."
&gt;
&gt; **2. Update**: "If the retrieved facts contain information that is already present in the memory but the information is **totally different**, then you have to update it. If the retrieved fact contains information that conveys the same thing as the elements present in the memory, then you have to **keep the fact which has the most information**."
&gt; - (a) memory "User likes to play cricket" + fact "Loves to play cricket with friends" → **UPDATE** (more info)
&gt; - (b) memory "Likes cheese pizza" + fact "Loves cheese pizza" → **no update** (same information)
&gt; - "keep the same ID"; "return the IDs in the output from the input IDs only and do not generate any new ID"
&gt;
&gt; **3. Delete**: "If the retrieved facts contain information that **contradicts** the information present in the memory, then you have to delete it."
&gt; - memory "Loves cheese pizza" + fact "Dislikes cheese pizza" → **DELETE**
&gt;
&gt; **4. No Change / NONE**

Output shape: `{"memory":[{"id","text","event":"ADD|UPDATE|DELETE|NONE","old_memory":"&lt;required if UPDATE&gt;"}]}`

Note the UPDATE example that *merges* rather than replaces: `"I really like cheese pizza"` + `"Loves chicken pizza"` → `"Loves cheese and chicken pizza"` (UPDATE, id preserved). That's a **coexist-by-merge** operation dressed as UPDATE.

**Mem0 v3 has changed strategy** — the repo now also ships an `ADDITIVE_EXTRACTION_PROMPT`: *"Your sole operation is ADD"*, with `linked_memory_ids` pointing at related/superseded memories, deferring adjudication to retrieval time **[V, in the same prompts.py file]**. Independently corroborated by TOKI's deployment scan: "mem0 v2 votes among add/update/delete/none labels and drops the loser; **mem0 v3 defers adjudication to retrieval**." **[V]**

Graph variant Mem0^g **[V]**: directed labeled graph `G=(V,E,L)`; "an LLM-based update resolver determines if certain relationships should be obsolete, **marking them as invalid rather than physically removing them** to enable temporal reasoning."

### 5.2 Engram — cheap-then-escalate cascade (the best template for you)

"Less Context, More Accuracy: A Bi-Temporal Memory Engine for LLM Agents," arXiv 2606.09900 **[V — read the PDF]** https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09900

Data model **[V]**:
- **Episode** — raw lossless turn/event, stamped with *event time* and *ingested-at*.
- **Fact** — atomic `(subject, predicate, object)` + surface text + embedding + **salience** + **confidence** + **provenance** (source episode ids) + `valid_at`/`invalid_at` (valid time) + `created_at`/`expired_at` (transaction time) + a **`supersedes` pointer**.
- Entities/Relations carry the same bi-temporal stamps.
- **Invariant: never hard-delete a contradicted fact — invalidate it (set `invalid_at`) and keep the history.**

**§3.4 Cheap-then-escalate conflict resolution, verbatim [V]:**

&gt; When a new fact arrives for an existing `(subject, predicate)` slot with a different object, Engram resolves it in increasing order of cost:
&gt; 1. **Slot match** (exact) signals a likely update; **embedding similarity** catches the same attribute under a different free-form predicate; **content subsumption** (one claim ⊆ the other) separates contradiction from elaboration.
&gt; 2. If the claims are clearly contradictory and temporally ordered, **invalidate the old one (`old.invalid_at ← new.valid_at`) and set `new.supersedes ← old.id`. No LLM call.**
&gt; 3. Only genuinely ambiguous cases escalate to an LLM adjudicator.

Architecture **[V]**: System-1 hot write path (append lossless episode, identity resolution across sessions/devices, light embedding, enqueue) — **no LLM, &lt;50 ms budget**. System-2 async consolidation — extract atomic facts, build the bi-temporal KG, detect conflicts + invalidate, score salience with decay/reinforcement, hierarchical abstraction (session summaries → profile). Read path: query decompose → 4 channels (dense, BM25, graph n-hop, recency/salience) → RRF fusion (+optional cross-encoder rerank) → bi-temporal as-of filter → abstention gate → assemble provenance-tagged context. Scoring:

`score(item|q) = w_sem·cos(q,item) + w_lex·bm25(q,item) + w_graph·prox(item) + w_rec·e^(−Δt/τ) + w_sal·sal(item)`

Numbers, LongMemEval_S 500 questions, official judge (DeepSeek-V3.2) **[V]**:

| System | Overall acc (95% CI) | Avg ctx tokens |
|---|---|---|
| Engram (lean) | **83.6%** [80.1, 86.6] | **9.6k** |
| engram_full (facts + full history) | 83.4% | 79k |
| full-context baseline | 73.2% [69.2, 76.9] | 79k |

+10.4 points at ~8× fewer tokens; paired McNemar exact p &lt; 10⁻⁶ vs full-context; lean vs full p = 0.91 (**structured facts carry all the gain; the full history adds tokens, not correctness**). Per category: **knowledge-update 87.5%** (most-recent-wins via invalidation), **temporal-reasoning 81.1%** (date-stamped context + as-of filtering), abstention 86.7%; headroom in multi-session aggregation 79.3% and single-session-preference 73.3%.

**Load-bearing negative finding [V]:** a **facts-only** read path *loses recall* — extraction drops detail some questions need verbatim. You must retrieve conflict-resolved facts **plus** the most relevant raw source chunks. *"We caution against shipping facts-only QA."* Directly applicable: keep your source blobs retrievable alongside your extracted facts.

### 5.3 Memanto — supersede / retain / annotate

arXiv 2604.22085 **[V — read the PDF]** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.22085

Typed semantic memory: **13 predefined semantic categories** (fact, preference, decision, commitment, … each with a Description, Example, and **Priority Signal** — e.g. `fact`: "Stable, high confidence"; `preference`: "Moderate decay"). **[V]** Typing gives (a) type-filtered retrieval, (b) implicit priority/decay signals for retrieval weighting.

Conflict resolution **[V]**: "operates through **semantic similarity matching against existing memories of the same type within the agent's namespace**, using a **configurable contradiction threshold**. When triggered, the system presents the agent with the conflicting memories and **three resolution options: `supersede` (replace the old memory), `retain` (keep the old memory), or `annotate` (preserve both with a conflict flag for human review)**."

Note also: "When a new memory is committed that semantically contradicts an existing memory … Memanto detects the conflict and **notifies the agent, requesting explicit resolution before the contradiction is persisted**." → *conflict as a first-class queue item*, exactly your review-queue design.

Temporal versioning **[V]**: three query modalities — **as-of** (state at historical timestamp, audit-trail reconstruction), **changed-since** (incremental context updates), **current-only** (non-superseded). "Memory supersession is non-destructive."

Results **[V]**: LongMemEval 89.8%, LoCoMo 87.1%, single retrieval query, sub-90 ms retrieval, no ingestion delay. It also cites MemoryAgentBench: "conflict resolution remains one of the most significant unsolved challenges in current memory systems, with **all evaluated methods failing on multi-hop conflict scenarios**."

### 5.4 Letta / MemGPT — no formal conflict semantics

MemGPT (arXiv 2310.08560) is an OS-metaphor paging system; there is **no contradiction resolution** — a "memory pressure" warning prompts the LLM to page/edit. **[V — read the PDF; grep for "contradict"/"conflict" returns nothing relevant.]**

Letta's shipped tools (`letta/functions/function_sets/base.py`) **[V, downloaded]**: `core_memory_append`, `core_memory_replace(label, old_content, new_content)`, `rethink_memory`, `memory_replace(label, old_string, new_string)`, `memory_insert(label, new_string, insert_line)`, `memory_apply_patch(label, patch)`, `memory_rethink(label, new_memory)`, `memory_finish_edits`. Docstring: *"allows you to replace a specific string in a memory block with a new string … used for making precise edits. Do NOT attempt to replace long strings."* Example: `memory_replace(label="human", old_string="Their name is Alice", new_string="Their name is Bob")`.

**That's it — conflict resolution is "the LLM does string surgery on a markdown block."** No valid time, no provenance, no audit row. TOKI's audit confirms: Letta admits belief-drift skew (N2) and audit erasure (N3). **[V]**

### 5.5 TOKI — the theory that types all four heuristics (best formal treatment)

"TOKI: A Bitemporal Operator Algebra for Contradiction Resolution in LLM-Agent Persistent Memory," Ziming Wang (HKUST), arXiv 2606.06240 **[V — read the full 43-page PDF]** https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06240 · code https://github.com/ZenAlexa/toki-bitemporal-memory

**Core reframing: contradiction resolution *is* write-time concurrency control.**

**Contradiction predicate [V]:** two facts contradict (`f₁ # f₂`) when they *"agree on subject and predicate, disagree on object, and their valid-time periods share a common instant."* Under closed-open `[t_from, t_to)` this holds for **9 of Allen's 13 base interval relations** — all except *before, after, meets, met-by* (the four sharing no interior instant).

**The four production heuristics, typed [V]:**

| Operator | Production strategy | Isolation precondition | Winner selector (source line) | Admitted anomaly |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `⊕t` | Last-writer-wins | Read-Committed | larger `(system_time_start, fact_id)` | P4 lost update |
| `⊕p` | Evidence-weighted | Snapshot Isolation | larger `(confidence, system_time_start, fact_id)` | A5B write skew |
| `⊕?` | Await-confirmation | RC + callback | external callback returns 0/1 | none |
| `⊕c` | Per-rule policy | SR on policy table | policy oracle returns 0/1 | precludes P3 phantom |

`⊕?` is literally your human review queue. `⊕c` is your rule table ("job title from HR system beats job title from a voice note").

**Dual-row schema (12 columns) [V]** — table `agent_memory`:

| # | Column | Role |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `fact_id` | PK part (with col. 7) |
| 2–4 | `subject`, `predicate`, `object` | fact content |
| 5–6 | `valid_from`, `valid_to` | valid-time period |
| 7–8 | `system_time_start`, `system_time_end` | system-time period (7 is PK part) |
| 9 | `provenance_id` | K-relation annotation `p ∈ P` |
| 10 | `confidence` | `[0,1]` |
| 11 | `resolution_strategy_id` | `∈ {⊕t, ⊕p, ⊕?, ⊕c}` |
| 12 | `row_kind` | hidden CHECK-constrained discriminator `∈ {current, audit}` |

Integrity constraints **[V]**: `CHECK (confidence BETWEEN 0.0 AND 1.0)`; `CHECK (row_kind IN ('current','audit'))`; PK `(fact_id, system_time_start)` so two system-time versions of one fact coexist; `CHECK (valid_from &lt; valid_to)`; `CHECK (system_time_start &lt; system_time_end)`. Open upper bound sentinel = `9999-12-31`. `as_of` predicate is closed-open (`system_time_start ≤ t &lt; system_time_end`), defaults `row_kind='current'`, accepts `kind='audit'|'all'`.

**Algorithm 1 — the write path, verbatim [V]:**

```
Require: fact f with strategy stamp strat(f), write time t_s, judge parameter pin θ;
         oracle ρ for ⊕? / ⊕c
 1: O ← open current rows on (subj,pred) with Ts end open      # row_kind = current
 2: if ∃ r ∈ O with r.obj = f.obj and overlap(r, f) then
 3:     return                                                  # duplicate confirmation
 4: end if
 5: C ← { r ∈ O : r # f }                                       # Allen overlap, differing object
 6: if C = ∅ then
 7:     insert f as a fresh current row; return
 8: else if |C| &gt; 1 then
 9:     raise                                                   # binary operators need one incumbent
10: end if
11: r ← the single incumbent in C
12: select operator ⊕ by strat(f): ⊕t|RC, ⊕p|SI, ⊕?|RC+cb, ⊕c|SR_policy
13: if ⊕ ∈ {⊕?, ⊕c} then
14:     v ← ρ(r, f); append v to judge_log[(R,θ)]                # BEFORE the operator commit
15: end if
16: (w, l) ← ⊕(r, f)
17: p_merge ← p_w ⊕ p_l                                         # exact K-semiring merge
18: if l = r then                                               # incumbent lost
19:     close r at Ts end t_s; insert w stamped with t_s and p_merge
20: end if
21: insert audit row (p_merge, strat(f), t_s, witness)          # row_kind = audit; defends N3
22: commit at the operator's isolation pin
23: return (w, audit)
```

Judge pinning: the decoder tuple is `θ = (prompt, seed, model, temperature, tool_hash)`; the judge verdict is logged **before** the operator commit, keyed by `(R, θ)`. That's what makes re-adjudication replay consistently.

**Three named agent-memory anomalies [V]:**
- **N1 replay inconsistency** — re-adjudicating a contradiction returns a different winner. Defence: **SR on a keyed judge log**.
- **N2 belief-drift skew** — write skew specialised to a `(subj, pred)` partition. Defence: **SR on the partition**.
- **N3 audit erasure** — loss of the overwritten fact. Defence: **the audit row**.

**Verdict matrix over 8 systems [V]** (A = admits, X = excludes, − = n/a; T = transcribed design, I = shipped code):

| System | N1 (T/I) | N2 (T/I) | N3 (T/I) |
|---|---|---|---|
| mem0 v2 | − / − | − / − | **A / A** |
| mem0 v3 | **A / A** | **A** / X | **A / A** |
| Graphiti | **A / A** | − / − | **A / A** |
| Letta | − / − | **A / A** | **A / A** |
| Zep | − / − | **A / A** | **A / A** |
| MIRIX | **A / A** | **A / A** | − / − |
| WorldDB (engine-layer) | X / X | X / X | X / X |
| **Toki** | X / X | X / X | X / X |

**Audit erasure is universal among the agent-memory baselines with a documented provenance path — five of six.** WorldDB only escapes by removing the LLM judge from the write path entirely.

Specific witness notes **[V]**: "Graphiti (T) N3: handler sets `invalid_at` on older edge, **emits no audit edge**"; "mem0 v2 (T) N3: **DELETE branch removes loser with no audit row**"; "mem0 v3 (T) N3: ADD-only accumulation shadows older fact at retrieval, no audit."

Effect sizes **[V]**: audit-row defence moves its LoCoMo slice by **Δ = +0.86** (paired-bootstrap CI [0.76, 0.94]); ablating the typed memory layer removes **0.49 accuracy** on 1,444 answerable LoCoMo questions. Cross-system comparison is explicitly underpowered (mem0 v3 Δ = −0.04 CI [−0.10,+0.00]; Graphiti −0.08 [−0.18,+0.00]; Zep +0.02 [−0.06,+0.10]) and claims no superiority. Cited external evidence: **BeliefShift leaves up to 42% of cross-session contradictions unresolved across seven LLM families**; **STALE scores the best frontier model at 55.2%**; **TSM recovers 12.2 accuracy points on LongMemEval/LoCoMo by separating dialogue time from occurrence time**.

### 5.6 Consolidated decision table

| Signal | Decision |
|---|---|
| Same `(subj,pred)`, same object, overlapping valid-time | **Duplicate confirmation** — merge provenance, bump confidence, no new row |
| Same `(subj,pred)`, different object, **non-overlapping** valid-time (Allen: before/after/meets/met-by) | **COEXIST** — two historical intervals, both true, no conflict |
| Same `(subj,pred)`, different object, overlapping valid-time, **temporally ordered** | **SUPERSEDE** — `old.invalid_at ← new.valid_at`; `new.supersedes ← old.id`; emit audit row. *No LLM call.* |
| Same slot, one object **subsumes** the other | **MERGE/elaborate** (Mem0's "keep the fact with the most information") |
| Different free-form predicate but high embedding sim + same subject | run the slot test on the resolved canonical predicate |
| Different objects, overlapping time, **not temporally ordered** (same timestamp, or both `valid_at` null) | **ESCALATE** → NLI, then LLM judge, then human queue |
| Multi-valued predicate by schema (e.g. `attended`, `skill`) | **COEXIST always** — never invalidate |
| Incoming fact is **older** than an existing candidate | insert **already invalidated** (`invalid_at ← candidate.valid_at`, `expired_at ← now`) |
| &gt;1 incumbent on the partition | **raise** rather than guess (TOKI line 8–9) |

## 6. Truth discovery / source reliability — implementable algorithms

### 6.1 The universal skeleton

From "A Survey on Truth Discovery," Li, Gao, Meng, Li, Su, Zhao, Fan, Han (SIGKDD Explorations 2016), arXiv 1505.02463 **[V — read the PDF]** https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.02463

```
Algorithm 1  General Procedure of Truth Discovery
Input:  claims {v_o^s} for objects o ∈ O from sources s ∈ S
Output: truths {v*_o}, source weights {w_s}
1: Initialize source weights {w_s}
2: repeat
3:   for o ← 1 to |O| do
4:     Truth computation: infer truth for object o given current {w_s}
5:   end for
6:   Source weight estimation: update {w_s} given current {v*_o}
7: until stop criterion satisfied
8: return {v*_o}, {w_s}
```

Three formalizations of the same fixed point **[V]**:
- **Iterative** (TruthFinder, Investment, Sums/Hubs-Authorities) — most interpretable.
- **Optimization**: `min_{ {w_s},{v*_o} } Σ_o Σ_s w_s · d(v_o^s, v*_o)` subject to a regularizer on `w`, solved by **coordinate descent** (→ the same two alternating steps). Distance `d` = 0-1 loss for categorical, L2 for continuous.
- **PGM**: likelihood `Π_s p(w_s|β) · Π_o [ p(v*_o|α) · Π_s p(v_o^s | v*_o, w_s) ]`, inferred by EM. With Gaussian `p(·)`, `v*_o` is the mean and `w_s` is the **precision** (1/variance).

Common stop criterion: change in `{v*}` or `{w}` below a threshold; TruthFinder/the QCRI benchmark use **cosine similarity between successive source-trust vectors ≤ δ**.

### 6.2 TruthFinder (Yin, Han, Yu — KDD 2007), exact equations

**[V — read the PDF]** http://hanj.cs.illinois.edu/pdf/kdd07_xyin.pdf

Four heuristics **[V]**: (1) usually only one true fact per property; (2) the true fact looks the same/similar across sites; (3) false facts on different sites are less likely to be similar; (4) a site that provides mostly true facts for many objects likely provides true facts for others.

```
(1)  t(w)  = Σ_{f ∈ F(w)} s(f) / |F(w)|                       # site trustworthiness
(2)  s(f)  = 1 − Π_{w ∈ W(f)} (1 − t(w))                      # naive-Bayes independence
(3)  τ(w)  = −ln(1 − t(w))                                    # trustworthiness SCORE, ∈ [0,∞)
(4)  σ(f)  = −ln(1 − s(f))                                    # confidence SCORE
(5)  σ(f)  = Σ_{w ∈ W(f)} τ(w)                                # Lemma 1: score is ADDITIVE
(6)  σ*(f) = σ(f) + ρ · Σ_{o(f')=o(f)} σ(f') · imp(f' → f)    # similar-fact influence
(7)  s*(f) = 1 − e^(−σ*(f))          → with damping: 1 − e^(−γ·σ*(f))
(8)  s(f)  = 1 / (1 + e^(−γ·σ*(f)))                           # logistic, keeps s(f) &gt; 0
```

Why (3)/(5) matter for implementation: the log transform turns a product into a **sum**, avoiding underflow *and* making trust differences meaningful — `t=0.9` vs `t=0.99` differ by 1.1× in `t` but **2×** in `τ`. **[V]**

- `ρ ∈ [0,1]` weights related-fact influence; **`imp(f' → f) &lt; 0` when `f` conflicts with `f'`** — this is where "contradicting evidence subtracts confidence" lives.
- `γ ∈ (0,1)` is the dampening factor compensating for **source dependence/copying** ("if a fact `f` is provided by five web sites with trustworthiness of 0.6, `f` will have confidence of 0.99!"). **[V]**
- The logistic in (8) prevents negative confidence when a fact is contradicted by trustworthy sources.

Initialization/convergence **[V]**: all sites start at uniform `t₀ = 0.9` (estimated average trustworthiness); stop when the cosine similarity between successive `t⃗` vectors changes by less than δ. Paper's params: **ρ = 0.5, γ = 0.3, δ = 0.001%**. Book-authors dataset: 894 bookstores, 1,265 books, 34,031 listings, avg **5.4 distinct author sets per book**. **TruthFinder reaches ~95% accuracy after the 3rd iteration and stabilizes; Voting is materially lower.** Timing: 8.73 s to pre-compute implications + 4.43 s for four iterations; Voting 1.22 s. Error breakdown on 100 books: correct — Voting 71, TruthFinder 85, Barnes &amp; Noble 64; missed author(s) — 12 / 2 / 4; incomplete names — 18 / 5 / 6. **[V]**

Implication function used: `imp(f₁→f₂) = z/x − base_sim` with `base_sim = 0.5` (partial-credit set overlap). **[V]**

### 6.3 CRH — the optimization framework (SIGMOD 2014)

Li, Li, Gao, Zhao, Fan, Han. **[V — read the PDF]** https://www.cse.buffalo.edu/~jing/doc/sigmod14_crh.pdf

```
min_{X*, W}  f(X*, W) = Σ_k w_k Σ_i Σ_m d_m(v*_im, v_im^(k))
             s.t.  δ(W) = 1,  W ∈ S
```

With regularizer `δ(W) = Σ_k exp(−w_k)`, **Theorem 1**: the problem is convex in `W` and the global optimum is

```
              (   Σ_i Σ_m d_m(v*_im, v_im^(k))          )
w_k = − log ( ─────────────────────────────────────────  )
              ( Σ_{k'} Σ_i Σ_m d_m(v*_im, v_im^(k'))    )
```

i.e. **a source's weight is the negative log of its share of total error.** Truth step: `v*_im ← argmin_v Σ_k w_k · d_m(v, v_im^(k))`. Block coordinate descent between the two. Different `d_m` per data type (0-1 for categorical, normalized L2 for continuous) is exactly the "heterogeneous" contribution — **and it's why CRH fits your schema, where a person has both categorical facts (employer) and numeric ones (headcount).** **[V]**

### 6.4 CATD — the fix for long-tail sources (VLDB 2015)

Li, Li, Gao, Su, Zhao, Demirbas, Fan, Han. **[V — read the PDF]** http://www.vldb.org/pvldb/vol8/p425-li.pdf

Problem: most sources make very few claims, so a point estimate `σ̂²_s` of a source's error variance is unreliable. CATD uses the **upper bound of the (1−α) chi-squared confidence interval** for `σ²_s` instead (minimax: minimize the worst plausible variance).

```
Σ_{n ∈ N_s} (x_n^s − x*_n)² / σ²_s  =  |N_s|·σ̂²_s / σ²_s  ~  χ²(|N_s|)

(1−α) CI for σ²_s = [ Σ(x_n^s − x*_n)² / χ²_(1−α/2,|N_s|) ,  Σ(x_n^s − x*_n)² / χ²_(α/2,|N_s|) ]

min_W Σ_s w_s² u_s²   s.t. Σ_s w_s = 1, w_s ≥ 0    ⟹    (7)  w_s ∝ 1/u_s² = χ²_(α/2,|N_s|) / Σ_{n ∈ N_s} (x_n^s − x*_n)²

x*_n = Σ_{s ∈ S_n} w_s · x_n^s / Σ_{s ∈ S_n} w_s
```

Their worked example, α = 0.05 **[V]**:

| Source | #claims | σ̂²_s | 95% CI | weight from σ̂² | **weight from u²** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 200 | 0.1 | (0.0830, 0.1229) | 0.4839 | **0.9385** |
| B | 200 | 3 | (2.4890, 3.6871) | 0.0161 | 0.0313 |
| C | **2** | 0.1 | (0.0271, **3.9498**) | 0.4839 | **0.0292** |
| D | **2** | 3 | (0.8133, **118.49**) | 0.0161 | 0.0010 |

Source C looks as good as A on the point estimate but gets 32× less weight once you account for its two observations. **This is exactly the correction you need**: a one-off voice note should not get the same source weight as a 200-fact email history. Categorical claims are one-hot encoded and squared L2 gives the error. Note: CATD's Algorithm 1 as published is **non-iterative** — remove outliers, initialize truths, compute weights via Eq(7), compute truths via Eq(1), return.

### 6.5 Dawid–Skene (1979) — confusion-matrix EM

**[V, via "Truth Inference in Crowdsourcing: Is the Problem Solved?" Zheng, Li, Li, Shan, Cheng, VLDB 2017]** http://www.vldb.org/pvldb/vol10/p541-zheng.pdf

Each worker/source `w` is an **ℓ×ℓ confusion matrix** `q^w`, where `q^w_{j,k} = Pr(worker answers k | truth is j)`. Answer likelihood `Pr(v_i^w | q^w, v*_i) = q^w_{v*_i, v_i^w}`. Objective:

```
Pr(V | {q^w}) = Π_{i=1..n} Σ_{z=1..ℓ}  Pr(v*_i = z) · Π_{w ∈ W_i} q^w_{z, v_i^w}
```

maximized by **EM** — E-step: posterior over each `v*_i` given current `{q^w}`; M-step: re-estimate each `q^w` from soft-labeled counts. Extensions: **LFC** adds Beta/Dirichlet priors `α^w_{j,k}` on `q^w`; **LFC_N** handles numeric tasks with `v_i^w ~ N(v*_i, σ²_w)`; **BCC/CBCC** are Bayesian versions (CBCC assigns each *community* a representative confusion matrix). **[V]**

Why D&amp;S matters for you specifically: unlike TruthFinder's single scalar trust, **a confusion matrix captures directional bias** — e.g. an email-signature parser that's great at emails but systematically wrong about job titles. If your sources are heterogeneous (meeting transcript vs. email vs. voice note vs. CRM), model reliability **per (source-type × predicate-type)**, not per source.

### 6.6 Knowledge-Based Trust (Google) — separating extraction error from source error

**[V, abstract]** arXiv 1502.03519, Dong, Gabrilovich, Murphy, Dang, Horn, Lugaresi, Sun, Zhang.

&gt; "We propose a way to distinguish errors made in the **extraction process** from **factual errors in the web source per se**, by using joint inference in a novel **multi-layer probabilistic model**. We call the trustworthiness score we computed Knowledge-Based Trust (KBT). … applied to a database of **2.8B facts** extracted from the web, and thereby estimate the trustworthiness of **119M webpages**."

The multi-layer trick is the important one for you: when a fact turns out to be wrong, you must decide whether *the source lied* or *your extractor misread it* — and you must estimate both jointly, or your source-reliability scores absorb your extractor's bugs. **Model `(source_reliability, extractor_precision)` as separate latent variables.**

Knowledge Vault (Dong et al., KDD 2014) is the companion system, fusing extractors + priors from an existing KB into calibrated per-triple probabilities. **[R, unverified — I did not fetch it.]**

### 6.7 Empirical reality check: how much does any of this beat Voting?

"Truth Discovery Algorithms: An Experimental Evaluation," Waguih &amp; Berti-Équille (QCRI TR, May 2014), arXiv 1409.6428 **[V — read the PDF]** https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.6428

Same convergence test for all (cosine similarity of successive source-trust vectors ≤ δ); all initialized `T_S = 0.8`.

**Book dataset** (877 sources, 33,235 claims, 1,263 objects, 1 attribute, gold on 100 objects):

| 2-Estimates | 0.9812 | 0.8893 | 0.8351 | 0.9748 | 4 | 0.193 | 124 |
| 3-Estimates | **0.9935** | 0.8849 | 0.8172 | **0.9915** | 42 | 1.474 | 1117 |
| SimpleLCA | 0.9758 | 0.9023 | 0.8610 | 0.9667 | 5 | 0.136 | 82 |
| GuessLCA | 0.9808 | 0.8820 | 0.8226 | 0.9748 | 17 | 0.566 | 82 |
| **AccuSim** | 0.9730 | **0.9516** | **0.9474** | 0.9583 | 3 | 10.092 | 2072 |

**Flight** (38 sources, 2.86M claims): MajorityVoting 0.8205 precision; best is Accu 0.9111, AccuSim 0.9049, Cosine 0.8825; **TruthFinder 0.7970 — *worse* than voting**; 3-Estimates 0.7028, SimpleLCA 0.6802.

**Weather** (16 sources, 365,890 claims): MajorityVoting 0.6305; best TruthFinder 0.6443, AccuNoDep 0.6442, SimpleLCA 0.6421; **AccuSim 0.5079 and Accu 0.5231 — well *below* voting.**

**Read that carefully.** No algorithm dominates. Voting's precision is within a couple of points of the best method on two of three datasets, and the sophisticated methods sometimes *lose*. The authors also found the tuned params from Book don't transfer: "parameters selected to maximize precision for the Book data set do not maximize precision of the algorithms when they are applied to other data sets." Runtime is brutal too: Depen/Accu/AccuSim scale **quadratically in #sources** (5,492–5,788 s in the scaling experiment) vs. MajorityVoting at 438–528 ms and TruthFinder at 1.912 s.

Pseudocode for TruthFinder, Cosine, 2-Estimates, 3-Estimates, LTM, MLE, Depen/Accu/AccuSim is all in that paper (Algorithms 2.1–2.7) — best single source if you want to implement more than one. Params they settled on: TruthFinder ρ=.5, γ=.1, T_S=.8, δ=.001; Depen α=.2, T_S=.8, c=.8; Accu c=.1; AccuSim c=.05; MLE β₁=.5, r=.5. **Ordering instability warning [V]:** Depen-family results fluctuate run-to-run because sources with equal dependence probability get arbitrary order (Depen precision .9814±.0002, Accu .9741±.0061, AccuSim .9413±.0051); they fixed it by using **lexical ordering** instead of dependence-probability ordering.

**Recommendation for you: start with confidence-weighted voting (`⊕p`), add CATD's chi-squared long-tail correction, and only reach for a full fixed-point iteration if you measure a gap.** Your source count is small (a handful of channel types), which is exactly the long-tail regime CATD targets and the regime where TruthFinder-style trust estimation is noisiest.

## 7. 2025–2026: confidence scoring, provenance-weighted fusion, consolidation conflict handling

**"Useful Memories Become Faulty When Continuously Updated by LLMs," arXiv 2605.12978** **[V, abstract-level]** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12978 — **the single most important paper for your nightly consolidation job.**
- Memory utility follows a **U-shape**: "first rises, then degrades, and **can fall below the no-memory baseline**."
- **GPT-5.4 fails on 54% of a set of ARC-AGI problems it had previously solved without memory.**
- The fault is in **consolidation, not the experiences**: "the same trajectories yield qualitatively different memories under different update schedules."
- Mitigations: **treat raw episodes as first-class evidence** and **gate consolidation explicitly rather than firing it after every interaction**; an episodic-only control that just retains trajectories "remains competitive with the consolidators."

Cross-corroborated by Engram's facts-only ablation finding. **Do not let your nightly job rewrite/overwrite atomic facts in place; make it emit new versions with supersession links, and keep the source blobs retrievable.**

**"STALE: Can LLM Agents Know When Their Memories Are No Longer Valid?", arXiv 2605.06527** **[V, abstract]** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06527
- Names the failure mode you'll hit most: **Implicit Conflict** — "a later observation invalidates an earlier memory **without explicit negation**, requiring contextual inference and commonsense reasoning to detect." (Your NLI model will miss most of these: no antonym, no negation, no numeric mismatch.)
- 400 expert-validated conflict scenarios, 1,200 queries, &gt;100 everyday topics, contexts to 150K tokens.
- Three probing dimensions: **State Resolution** (detect the belief is outdated), **Premise Resistance** (reject queries that falsely presuppose stale state), **Implicit Policy Adaptation** (apply the update to future behavior).
- **Best evaluated model: 55.2% overall.** Models frequently accept outdated assumptions embedded in the user's query.
- Prototype **CUPMem** uses "structured state consolidation and propagation-aware search"; conclusion: **explicit state adjudication** is the promising direction.

**BeliefShift, arXiv 2603.23848** **[V, abstract]** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23848 — 2,400 human-annotated multi-session trajectories (health, politics, personal values, product preferences); 7 models (GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, Gemini 1.5 Pro, LLaMA-3, Mistral-Large) zero-shot + RAG. Metrics: **Belief Revision Accuracy (BRA), Drift Coherence Score (DCS), Contradiction Resolution Rate (CRR), Evidence Sensitivity Index (ESI)**. Core finding: "models that personalize aggressively resist drift poorly, while factually grounded models miss legitimate belief updates." Up to **42% of cross-session contradictions left unresolved**.

**TSM — "Beyond Dialogue Time: Temporal Semantic Memory for Personalized LLM Agents," arXiv 2601.07468** **[V, abstract]** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07468 — two named problems: **temporal inaccuracy** ("memories are organized by dialogue time rather than their actual occurrence time") and **temporal fragmentation** (missing durative/persistent-state info). Fix: semantic timelines + **durative memory** consolidating temporally continuous, semantically related info + query-aware retrieval with temporal intent. **Up to 12.2% absolute accuracy improvement** on LongMemEval and LoCoMo. → **Your `occurred_at` must be distinct from your `captured_at`.** A meeting on Tuesday discussing something that happened last March produces a fact with `valid_at = March`, not Tuesday.

**ODKE+ (Apple), arXiv 2509.04696** **[V, abstract]** https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.04696 — production open-domain KG extraction pipeline with the exact stage decomposition you want: (1) **Extraction Initiator** detects missing/stale facts, (2) **Evidence Retriever**, (3) hybrid extractors (pattern rules + ontology-guided LLM prompting), (4) **lightweight Grounder validates extracted facts using a second LLM**, (5) **Corroborator ranks and normalizes candidates for ingestion**. Numbers: **9M+ Wikipedia pages, 19M high-confidence facts, 98.8% precision, 195 predicates, up to 48% overlap improvement with third-party KGs, ~50-day average reduction in update lag.** (Formulas/thresholds are in the full paper, not the abstract.)

**"Always-On Agents: A Survey of Persistent Memory, State, and Governance in LLM Agents," arXiv 2606.30306** **[V — read the PDF]** https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30306 — best synthesis of this whole space.
- "The failure modes of semantic facts are **staleness, contradiction, and authority confusion**."
- The deepest problem it names is **authority confusion**: "a benchmark that scores recall rewards an agent for retrieving a fact regardless of whether it **still carries a current, unrevoked authority to influence action**. … an agent can top a preference-following benchmark and still have no mechanism to revoke a fact the user has retracted." Directly relevant to your GDPR erasure work — *revocation is not deletion.*
- **DynamicMem** (Wang, Braverman, Payani, Zheng, Liu, 2026) shows user attributes/habits/preferences drift on **different timelines** driven by external events, "so a single decay rate cannot keep a semantic store current."
- **CarMem** (Kirmayr, Stappen, Schneider, Matthes, André, 2025), arXiv 2501.09645 **[V, abstract]** https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.09645 — category-bounded preference memory for voice assistants; **bounding facts to declared categories lets scheduled maintenance sharply cut redundant and contradictory stored preferences**, and it's motivated by EU privacy/transparency requirements. **This is the closest published analogue to your typed people/orgs/engagements schema plus nightly consolidation.**
- On the imports: "**Park [2026] builds a graph-native versioned memory whose update and contraction operations are proven to satisfy the AGM and Hansson belief-base postulates, importing rationality guarantees that the heuristic consolidation literature lacks entirely, and Wang [2026b] recasts contradiction resolution as write-time concurrency control** … giving the both-truths representation an always-on agent needs to mark a fact superseded rather than delete it."
- On graph substrates generally: "few works address edge deletion, node versioning, graph-integrity validation under concurrent agent-driven updates, or **who is authorized to rewrite a relation**."

Others worth a look, verified only as titles/abstracts:
- **Governed Shared Memory for Multi-Agent LLM Systems**, arXiv 2606.24535 — names four failure modes (unauthorized leakage, stale propagation, **contradiction persistence**, provenance collapse) and four primitives (scoped retrieval, **temporal supersession**, provenance tracking, policy-governed propagation); production service MemClaw reconstructed 100% of depth-four derivation chains. **[V, abstract]** https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24535
- **ShadowMerge**, arXiv 2605.09033 — poisoning attack on graph memory via **relation-channel conflicts**: a poisoned relation shares the same query-activated anchor and canonicalized relation channel as benign evidence while carrying a conflicting value. Your `(subject, predicate)` partition key is also an attack surface. **[V, abstract]** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09033
- **MemLineage** (2605.14421), **HaluMem** (2511.03506), **Memanto** (2604.22085), **APEX-MEM** (2604.14362), **WorldDB** (2604.18478), **MIRIX/MMA** (2602.16493). **[V, citations from TOKI's bibliography]**

## 8. Concrete recommendation for your MongoDB knowledge brain

Synthesizing across TOKI (formal contract), Engram (cost cascade), Memanto (three-way resolution + typed categories), Nous (reliability signals), CATD (long-tail weighting), and Graphiti (temporal guards):

**Schema.** On your facts collection, per fact document: `subject`, `predicate`, `object`, `valid_from`, `valid_to`, `system_time_start`, `system_time_end` (sentinel `9999-12-31`), `confidence ∈ [0,1]`, `provenance[]` (source ids + channel tier), `resolution_strategy`, `supersedes` (fact_id), `row_kind ∈ {current, audit}`. Unique index on `(fact_id, system_time_start)`. Partial index on `{subject:1, predicate:1}` **filtered to `row_kind:'current'` and open `system_time_end`** — that's your partition-scan index for the write path. Add a partial index on `{row_kind:'current', valid_from:1, valid_to:1}` for as-of queries.

**Predicate cardinality declaration.** Tag each predicate in your schema as `single_valued` (employer, title, email) vs `multi_valued` (attended, skill, mentioned_topic). **Multi-valued predicates never conflict.** This one declaration kills the majority of spurious conflict work.

**Write path.** Implement TOKI Algorithm 1 verbatim. Use `⊕p` (evidence-weighted, `(confidence, system_time_start, fact_id)`) as your default operator, `⊕?` (await-confirmation) as your review-queue operator, `⊕c` (per-rule policy) for hard rules (e.g. CRM/HR-sourced fields always win over voice notes). Always emit the audit row — five of six shipped systems get this wrong.

**Conflict cascade.** Engram's ordering: slot match → embedding sim on the predicate → subsumption test → Allen-overlap test → (only if still ambiguous) NLI → (only in the ambiguous band) LLM judge → (only if the judge is low-confidence or the rule table says so) human queue. Most facts should reach a decision with **zero model calls**.

**Confidence.** `r = min(channel_tier, content_confidence)` (Nice's provenance cap; content-only trust is 100%-poisonable). Weight sources with CATD's `w_s ∝ χ²_(α/2,|N_s|) / Σ(error²)` so a source with 3 facts can't outvote one with 300. Track reliability **per (channel × predicate-type)** à la Dawid–Skene, not per channel.

**Retrieval.** Enforce the exclusion at the query layer, not the app layer — Kumiho's mandatory `WHERE NOT deprecated` clause on **both** the text and vector branches. In Mongo that means the `row_kind`/`system_time_end` filter must live inside the `$search`/`$vectorSearch` `filter` stage, not in a downstream `$match` you might forget.

**Nightly consolidation.** Gate it (2605.12978: firing after every interaction is what breaks it). Never overwrite atomic facts in place — emit versions. Keep raw episodes/blobs first-class and retrievable alongside facts (Engram: facts-only loses recall). Consider running your consolidation only on partitions that received writes since the last run, and log a before/after diff so a bad run is revertible via as-of read.

**Evaluation.** Build a small internal analogue of STALE: 50–100 hand-built implicit-conflict scenarios from your own domain (someone changes job, an engagement ends, a preference is retracted). The published numbers say the best frontier models sit at ~55% here; assume yours will too until measured. Watch the **knowledge-update** and **temporal-reasoning** slices specifically — Zep's own data shows knowledge-update can *regress* with a weaker model.

### 2.1 Graphiti's bi-temporal model (the reference implementation)

Everything below is `[V-direct]` from `graphiti_core` at commit `7cf0cab`, v0.29.3, 2026-08-01.

**Four timestamps, two axes.** On `EntityEdge`:
- `created_at` — when the system learned it (transaction time start)
- `expired_at` — when the system learned it was no longer true (transaction time end)
- `valid_at` — when the fact became true in the world (event time start)
- `invalid_at` — when it stopped being true in the world (event time end)
- plus `reference_time` — the episode's timestamp that produced this edge, and `episodes: list[str]` — provenance to the raw episodes

**The write path, precisely.** For each extracted edge, Graphiti retrieves **two different candidate sets**:

1. **Duplicate candidates** = `EntityEdge.get_between_nodes(source_uuid, target_uuid)` — every existing edge between *the same directed node pair* — then ranked by hybrid search over the extracted fact text.
2. **Invalidation candidates** = unfiltered hybrid search over the whole graph (`EDGE_HYBRID_SEARCH_RRF`, limit 10) for the same fact text, minus anything already in set 1.

Both lists go into **one** LLM call with **continuous index numbering across both lists**, returning:

```python
class EdgeDuplicate(BaseModel):
    duplicate_facts: list[int]     # only from EXISTING FACTS range
    contradicted_facts: list[int]  # from either list
```

The prompt's decisive examples:
- `"Alice works at Acme as a software engineer"` vs `"...as a senior engineer"` → `duplicate_facts=[], contradicted_facts=[1]` — "same relationship but updated title — contradiction, NOT a duplicate"
- `"Bob ran 5 miles on Tuesday"` vs `"Bob ran 3 miles on Wednesday"` → both empty — "different events on different days"

**Then the temporal arithmetic** (`resolve_edge_contradictions`), which is the part people get wrong:

```python
# Skip if the windows don't actually overlap:
if (edge.invalid_at &lt;= new.valid_at) or (new.invalid_at &lt;= edge.valid_at):
    continue
# Only supersede if the old edge started EARLIER:
elif edge.valid_at &lt; new.valid_at:
    edge.invalid_at = new.valid_at          # close event-time window
    edge.expired_at = edge.expired_at or utc_now()   # close transaction-time window
```

**And the inverse case, which is the genuinely clever bit.** Before checking contradictions, Graphiti checks whether the *new* edge should be born already-expired — for out-of-order/backfilled ingestion:

```python
if resolved_edge.expired_at is None:
    invalidation_candidates.sort(key=lambda c: (c.valid_at is None, ensure_utc(c.valid_at)))
    for candidate in invalidation_candidates:
        if candidate.valid_at &gt; resolved_edge.valid_at:
            resolved_edge.invalid_at = candidate.valid_at
            resolved_edge.expired_at = now
            break   # "Expire new edge since we have information about more recent events"
```

Timestamps themselves come from a separate small-model call (`extract_edges.extract_timestamps`) given only `{fact, reference_time}` — cheap and isolated.

**What Zep hands the agent.** The context block renders facts as
`(2024-11-15 14:23:00 - present) [CONTACTED_SUPPORT_ABOUT] &lt;Emily contacted support regarding API rate limits&gt;`
and entities as `{ name: X, types: [...], summary: Y }`. Configurable via `%{edges limit=N types=[...]}`, `%{entities}`, `%{observations limit=N}` (capped 20), `%{thread_summaries}` (capped 20).

**Zep "observations"** are productized reflection: cross-entity, evidence-backed patterns detected from graph structure, auto-deduped and merged when new evidence aligns — distinct from single-edge facts and single-node summaries.

**Applied to your schema.** You have `supersededBy` (a pointer, transaction-time-ish) and `validUntil` (event-time end) — and my read of `packages/knowledge/` confirms **`validUntil` is declared in `schemas/facts.ts` and `currentlyValidFilter` but never written by any code path.** You are missing `validFrom` and a `supersededAt` timestamp. Adding those four fields is what lets you answer "what was true on date X" and "what did we believe on date X" as separate queries — which is exactly what LongMemEval's knowledge-update category and GroupMemBench probe.

### 2.2 Graphiti's entity resolution ladder (deterministic-first, LLM-last)

`[V-direct]` from `dedup_helpers.py` + `node_operations.py`. This runs **before** any LLM call and resolves the majority of cases for free:

1. **Exact normalized-name match** (`lower()`, collapse whitespace). Exactly 1 hit → resolve. **More than 1 hit → escalate to LLM** (ambiguity is a signal, not a tie-break).
2. **Entropy gate**, guarding the fuzzy path only: reject if `len &lt; 6 AND tokens &lt; 2`, or if Shannon character entropy `&lt; 1.5`.
3. **MinHash + LSH**: 3-gram shingles over `[a-z0-9' ]`, 32 blake2b-seeded permutations, banded at size 4 (8 bands).
4. **Jaccard ≥ 0.90** on the shingle sets → resolve.
5. Otherwise → LLM dedup pass with the candidate list.

Bulk ingestion adds union-find with path compression (`_build_directed_uuid_map`) to collapse transitive duplicate chains, plus a Redis "recent dedup cache" override so edges resolved seconds ago but not yet visible in the search index still participate.

**Guardrail worth copying:** `apply_capped_attributes` caps LLM-emitted attribute strings at **250 chars** (`GRAPHITI_ATTRIBUTE_MAX_LENGTH`) and **drops** over-length values, falling back to the prior value — rather than truncating. Prevents slow schema drift.

### 2.3 Generative Agents' reflection — and the paper/code discrepancies

`[V-direct]` from `joonspk-research/generative_agents`. **Three things the paper says are not what the code does:**

| | Paper | Released code |
|---|---|---|
| Weights | "all αs are set to 1" | `gw = [0.5, 3, 2]` on top of `w=1` → **relevance×3, importance×2, recency×0.5** |
| Decay | 0.995 | `recency_decay = 0.99` |
| Recency basis | hours since last access | `decay ** i` where **i is the rank index** in the chronologically sorted candidate list |

If you implement from the paper you get materially different behavior from the demo everyone saw. Relevance dominates.

**Reflection, verified from `reflect.py`:**
- Trigger is a **countdown**: `importance_trigger_curr` starts at `importance_trigger_max = 150` and decrements by each new event's poignancy; fires at ≤ 0. Roughly 2–3×/day.
- `generate_focal_points(persona, n=3)` builds the prompt from the **last `importance_ele_n` records** (events since the last reflection), not a fixed 100.
- Each focal point runs `new_retrieve(..., n_count=30)`, then `generate_insights_and_evidence(persona, nodes, n=5)` → 5 insights, **each with the node IDs that were its evidence**.
- Insights re-enter the memory stream as thoughts with their own poignancy and embedding → recursive reflection trees.
- **`expiration = created + 30 days` on every thought.** A TTL on derived memories that almost nobody mentions.

**Poignancy prompt, verbatim:** *"On the scale of 1 to 10, where 1 is purely mundane (e.g., brushing teeth, making bed) and 10 is extremely poignant (e.g., a break up, college acceptance), rate the likely poignancy of the following piece of memory."*

Ablation (TrueSkill μ): full 29.89 → no reflection 26.88 → no reflection+planning 25.64 → no memory/planning/reflection 21.21. Human baseline 22.95.

### 2.4 A-MEM's linking and memory evolution

`[V-direct]` from `agiresearch/A-mem`. Note the repo's last commit is **2025-12-12** — this is research code, not a maintained system.

Note structure: `content, timestamp, keywords[], tags[], context, links[], evolution_history[], retrieval_count, last_accessed`.

`process_memory(note)`:
1. `find_related_memories(note.content, **k=5**)`
2. One LLM call with the evolution prompt returning:
```json
{"should_evolve": bool,
 "actions": ["strengthen", "update_neighbor"],
 "suggested_connections": ["neighbor_ids"],
 "tags_to_update": [...],
 "new_context_neighborhood": ["...per neighbor, in input order..."],
 "new_tags_neighborhood": [[...], [...]]}
```
The prompt hard-constrains array lengths to the neighbor count. `strengthen` links the new note; `update_neighbor` **rewrites the existing neighbours' `context` and `tags`** in place — backward-propagating revision.

Separately, `evo_cnt % evo_threshold == 0` (threshold **100**) triggers `consolidate_memories()`, which rebuilds the retriever index.

**Transfer:** the "when you write a new fact, let it revise the *description* of nearby facts" move is the cheapest form of consolidation available and doesn't require merging anything.

### 2.5 mem0's update-op classifier — and why they deleted it

`[V-agent]`, from source at both v0.1.118 and `main`. This is the design most people mean by "mem0," and **it no longer exists.**

The old two-phase design:
- Extraction: `FACT_RETRIEVAL_PROMPT` → `{"facts": [...]}`
- For **each** extracted fact independently: `vector_store.search(limit=5)` → union, dedupe by id
- **UUID→integer remap before the prompt** (source comment: `# Map UUIDs to integers (anti-hallucination)`), mapped back after
- One LLM call → `{"memory": [{"id", "text", "event": "ADD|UPDATE|DELETE|NONE", "old_memory"}]}`
- DELETE = "the retrieved facts contain information that **contradicts**" existing

PR #4805 (2026-04-14) replaced all of it with a single-call, **ADD-only** pipeline. `grep -ic graph mem0/memory/main.py` on `main` returns **0**. The migration guide states plainly: *"Graph store support has been removed entirely"*, `add()` *"Returns ADD only"*.

**That's the finding, not the mechanism.** The vendor with the most production mileage on LLM-diffed memory shipped monotonic accumulation and pushed conflict resolution into ranking. Known failure modes of the old design: hallucinated IDs silently dropping ops (every branch wrapped in bare `except`); DELETE conflating "changed their mind" with "has two of these"; lossy UPDATE via the "keep the fact which has the most information" heuristic.

Two things still worth stealing:
- **The UUID→integer remap.** Cheap, general, applies to any prompt where the LLM must reference existing records.
- **`ADDITIVE_EXTRACTION_PROMPT`** (~470 lines) — specifically the Observation-Date-vs-Current-Date grounding, "contextually rich, not atomic," never-generalize-proper-nouns ("promoted to assistant manager" → keep "assistant manager"), and explicit anti-patterns for meta-extraction ("WRONG: 'User shared a case summary'") and echo-extraction.

One trap to avoid: their hybrid fusion computes `max_possible` **globally per query** (`1.0 + 1.0 if any bm25 + 0.5 if any entity`), so scores aren't comparable across queries and a fixed `threshold` won't transfer. Use RRF or per-candidate normalization.

### 2.6 (Bonus) The 2026 result that should change your write path

`[V-agent]` — **arXiv:2606.15903**, *"Control-Plane Placement Shapes Forgetting"* (Dongxu Yang, June 2026). I confirmed the paper is real. Thesis: **production failures are predominantly *forgetting* failures, not recall failures, yet every existing benchmark measures recall.**

Three placement regimes, tested across 13 configurations on **ForgetEval** (1,000 templated + 385 adversarial cases, Fleiss' κ = 0.958, MIT-licensed, 6-method adapter protocol):

| Where the LLM sits | Wins | Loses |
|---|---|---|
| **Nowhere** (deterministic) | lexical + temporal ≥95%, `temporal_qualifier` 37/37 | `identifier_obfuscation` **5%**, `cross_lingual` **0%** |
| **Inscribe-time** (during extraction) | canonicalization **100%** | intent-aware deletion **0%** |
| **Mutation-time hook** (during supersede/purge planning) | intent-aware deletion **78–85%**, overall **91.7–94.2%** | ~2.3 s/mutation vs 64–191 ms |

The regimes are **complementary, not redundant.** And the cost is trivial for a nightly job: ~$0.00044 per mutation call, and **recall latency is unchanged** — the hook only fires on writes.

The axis-flip result: **MemPalace scores 0/385 on ForgetEval-Adv and 40% on a recall benchmark** — mid-pack on recall, catastrophic on forgetting. Graphiti scores **7.0%** and HippoRAG **9.5%** on ForgetEval, because neither exposes deletion primitives. Recall rankings are close to the inverse of forgetting rankings.

Caveat the authors state themselves: 253/385 adversarial cases were drafted by DeepSeek-V3, admitted by a Qwen judge, and the winning hook is also DeepSeek-V3. On externally authored cases the deterministic band drops from 63–68% to 28–51%. The *directional* finding is solid; absolute numbers are soft.

## 3. Patterns catalog

**P1 — Bi-temporal supersession.** Four timestamps: two for event time (`valid_at`/`invalid_at`), two for transaction time (`created_at`/`expired_at`). Contradiction closes the old window rather than deleting the row. Enables "as of" queries on both axes and makes backfill correct. *Graphiti, Zep, mem0^g (paper only), Supermemory (partial).*

**P2 — Two candidate sets, one adjudication call.** Retrieve *duplicate* candidates (scope-constrained: same entity pair / same anchor) and *contradiction* candidates (broad semantic search) separately, then present both to one LLM call with continuous indexing. Scoping the duplicate set is what keeps this tractable — Zep's paper says so explicitly. *Graphiti.*

**P3 — Deterministic-first resolution ladder.** Exact match → cheap fuzzy (MinHash/LSH + Jaccard) → LLM only for the residue and for *ambiguous* exact matches. Treat ambiguity as an escalation signal, not a tie-break. *Graphiti, Cognee (UUID5 from identity fields).*

**P4 — Reflection over an importance budget.** Accumulate an importance/poignancy counter; fire consolidation when it crosses a threshold, not on a fixed clock. Generate a small number of questions, retrieve per question, synthesize insights **with citations to the evidence records**. *Generative Agents (threshold 150), A-MEM (every 100 notes), Honcho (≥50 conclusions AND ≥8h AND 60-min idle).*

**P5 — Backward-propagating revision.** A new memory doesn't just get linked; it triggers rewriting of its neighbours' *descriptions and tags*. Cheaper than merging and doesn't destroy anything. *A-MEM, Graphiti entity summaries, Zep observations.*

**P6 — Dual watermarks on incremental consolidation.** `[V-direct]` from Graphiti's `SagaNode`: `last_summarized_at` is **wall-clock** and is the *filter* watermark (pick up anything whose `created_at` &gt; this, so backfilled items with old event times still get caught); `last_summarized_episode_valid_at` is the **event-time** watermark that answers "how fresh is this summary?" Two fields with deliberately different semantics. Directly applicable to your nightly job.

**P7 — Deltas merged by non-LLM logic.** Never let the LLM rewrite the whole store. Emit itemized deltas with stable IDs and helpful/harmful counters; merge deterministically. ACE (arXiv:2510.04618) measured the failure mode: **18,282 tokens → 122 tokens in one step, accuracy 66.7 → 57.1 — below the 63.7 no-adaptation baseline.** *ACE, Anthropic Dreams (writes to a new store, never mutates the input).*

**P8 — Mutation-time LLM hook.** Put the LLM in the *planning* of supersede/purge/release, not just in extraction. It's the only placement that recovers intent-aware deletion, and it costs nothing on the read path. *ForgetEval (arXiv:2606.15903).*

**P9 — Facts as index keys over verbatim values.** LongMemEval's ablation: replacing rounds with extracted facts *hurts* (information loss), but indexing `K = V + fact` beats both — Recall@5 0.582→**0.644**, Recall@10 0.692→**0.784**, QA 0.615→**0.657**. Extract for retrieval, keep the source for grounding.

**P10 — RRF-fused hybrid + optional cross-encoder.** Dense + BM25 → RRF → rerank. Constants in the wild: Graphiti `rank_const=1`, Letta/Turbopuffer `k=60` (0.5/0.5 weights), MongoDB `$rankFusion` **hardcoded 60**. Nobody uses cosine alone anymore.

**P11 — Score-shaped recency, not filtered recency.** Redis Agent Memory Server publishes the only fully-specified formula:
```
final    = 0.8·semantic + 0.2·recency
recency  = 0.6·freshness + 0.4·novelty
freshness = exp(-ln2 · days_since_created     / 30)
novelty   = exp(-ln2 · days_since_last_access /  7)
```
Apache-2.0, and it maps directly onto a `$score` stage inside `$scoreFusion`. Related: MemoryBank's `R = exp(-t/S)` with `S += 1` on recall. *(Note `[V-direct]`: MemoryBank's reference implementation has an operator-precedence bug — `math.exp(-t / 5*S)` evaluates as `exp(-(t/5)·S)`, so higher strength causes **faster** forgetting. Intended: `exp(-t/(5*S))`. And forgetting is stochastic: `if random.random() &gt; retention: forget`.)*

**P12 — Decay as a multiplier, expiry as a filter-out-of-view.** mem0's platform decay never zeroes a candidate (worst case 0.3×); `expiration_date` hides but doesn't delete. Preserves recoverability.

**P13 — Provenance as a first-class queryable join.** MemMachine's `citations(profile_id → episode_id)` table; Graphiti's `EntityEdge.episodes[]`; Honcho's Dialectic tracing a conclusion back to its premises; Auto-Dreamer giving the consolidator a `get_source_trace` tool. Your `sources ↔ facts ↔ derivedFrom` is the same idea.

**P14 — Provenance semirings, not flat lineage.** `derivedFrom: ObjectId[]` is `Lineage(X)` — it cannot distinguish AND from OR. To answer "does this derived fact survive if I delete source S?", you need at least `PosBool(X)`: DNF nesting `derivedFrom: ObjectId[][]`, or a `derivation: "merge" | "corroboration" | "inference"` discriminator. (Green/Karvounarakis/Tannen, PODS 2007.)

**P15 — Non-destructive consolidation output.** Anthropic's Dreams writes a **brand-new memory store** and never mutates the input; you review and discard. Immutable memory *versions* (`memver_…`) plus a **`redact` endpoint** on them — the only published erasure-with-audit primitive from any vendor.

**P16 — Runtime-driven persistence, not tool-driven.** MongoDB's own `@mongodb-developer/vercel-ai-memory` README says tool-driven session writes are *"not deterministic: the model sometimes skips writes."* Persist from hooks. Corollary from Mastra: run memory processors **after** output guardrails, so an aborting guardrail prevents persistence entirely.

**P17 — Two-tier context loading.** Always-in-context core + tree-and-descriptions visible with contents fetched on demand. Letta after three years landed on `grep` over Markdown for the second tier and dropped the vector index. OpenViking compiles every entry to L0 (~100 tok) / L1 (~2k) / L2 (full).

**P18 — Absolute-date normalization at write time.** Letta's sleep-time prompt: *"do not write 'today' or 'recently'... because 'today' and 'recently' are relative, and the memory is persisted indefinitely."* mem0's extraction prompt grounds against an explicit `## Observation Date`. Trivial to implement, high value.

**P19 — Attributed beliefs are not contradictions.** "Anna says the deadline is tight" vs "Petra says it's comfortable" is two facts, not a conflict. Honcho makes this structural with `(observer_id, observed_id)`. GroupMemBench shows this is where team-context systems collapse — best system 27.1% on knowledge-update.

**P20 — Contradiction as an edge, resolution deferred.** Cognee logs a WARNING and writes a `contradicts` edge; nothing is rewritten. Your review queue *is* this pattern — TOKI (arXiv:2606.06240) types it as the **await-confirmation** operator, the strongest of four for correctness.

**P21 — Keyed judge logging.** TOKI's sharp result: *"keyed logging of the adjudicating judge is necessary for replay consistency, a discipline every deployed baseline we audit omits."* Log model + version + prompt hash + verdict, committed **before** the operator commits. Otherwise re-adjudicating the same contradiction can return a different winner.

**P22 — Exactly-once string matching on edits.** Letta's `memory_replace` requires `old_string` to occur exactly once and errors with line numbers otherwise; legacy `core_memory_replace` used `str.replace()` and silently replaced all occurrences. Anthropic's memory tool specifies the same strict behavior. Copy the strict one.

## 4. Evaluation and benchmarks

### The state of the field

**LOCOMO is broken and everyone knows it.** Verified by direct dataset inspection: the public `locomo10.json` has 1,986 questions, of which **446 are category 5 (adversarial) and 444 of those have no `answer` field at all** — they carry `adversarial_answer`, which is *the answer the model must not give*. A harness keying on `answer` silently drops them; a harness keying on `adversarial_answer` as gold **rewards hallucination**. That is the mechanical explanation for the Zep↔Mem0 dispute, where Mem0 alleged Zep's 84% excluded cat-5 from the denominator while counting cat-5 correct answers in the numerator (recomputed: 58.44%).

Additional measured defects: 99/1,540 wrong golden answers (6.4%, capping any system at ~93.57%); a gpt-4o-mini judge that **accepted 62.81% of deliberately wrong "vague-but-topical" answers**; conversations of only 16k–26k tokens, so full-context wins.

Two independent results that should end the argument:
- **Letta**: a plain filesystem with `grep`/`search_files` on **gpt-4o-mini** scores **74.0%**, beating mem0's best graph variant (68.5%). Their own conclusion: current memory benchmarks may not be meaningful.
- **Mem0's own paper**: full-context **72.90** beats Mem0 **66.88** and Mem0^g **68.44**. The paper's real claim is cost/latency, which marketing flattens into "SOTA."

**Independent reproduction gap:** Maximem's standardized harness reproduces Mem0's published LongMemEval **93.4% at 73.8%**, attributing the delta to dataset-specific equivalence rules mapped to public question IDs, judge prompts instructing "lean toward yes," and asymmetric verification gates (five steps required before marking WRONG, none before marking CORRECT).

### Statistical power — the thing nobody accounts for

Computed on verified counts (Wilson 95% CI, two-proportion at 80% power):

| Slice | n | CI half-width | Min detectable difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| LongMemEval overall | 500 | ±4.4 | 8.7 pts |
| temporal-reasoning | 133 | ±8.4 | 16.8 |
| **knowledge-update** | **78** | ±10.8 | **22.0** |
| **abstention** | **30** | ±16.8 | **35.4** |
| LoCoMo cats 1–4 | 1,540 | ±2.5 | 4.9 |

**The two categories that matter most for your product — knowledge-update and abstention — are the two least-powered in the benchmark.** Zep's "knowledge update +6.52%" is noise on n=78.

For your own golden set (paired design, McNemar): 93 per arm to detect 20 pts, 170 for 15 pts, 388 for 10 pts.

### What's actually current in mid-2026

| Purpose | Use | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Regression smoke test | LoCoMo cats 1–4 | never cat 5 |
| Knowledge update / abstention | **LongMemEval-S (`longmemeval-cleaned`)** | the original haystack contained contradicting sessions |
| Contradiction at scale | **BEAM** (ICLR 2026), MemoryAgentBench FactConsolidation | see below |
| **Your product shape** | **GroupMemBench**, **GateMem** | multi-party workplace threads; multi-principal governance |
| Deletion / GDPR | **ForgetEval** (MIT, 6-method adapter) | nothing else tests it |
| Does memory help the *task* | **MemoryArena** (ICML 2026), **STATE-Bench** (Microsoft, MIT) | the 2026 shift |

**The three numbers that should reframe your priorities:**

1. **BEAM, Contradiction Resolution: 0.006–0.050 across every model and method.** Effectively zero, while the same systems score 0.3–0.8 on retrieval. On MemoryAgentBench multi-hop conflict resolution, everything maxes out at 6%.
2. **GroupMemBench (multi-party workplace threads, the closest published analogue to your system):** BM25 43.22 avg &gt; HippoRAG 39.72 &gt; A-Mem 35.10 &gt; MemGPT 28.15 &gt; **Mem0 25.73** &gt; GraphRAG 20.56. Mem0 scores **4.67%** on knowledge-update. The authors: *"current memory ingestion erases the structural and lexical features group memory depends on."* Notably, **abstention stays &gt;80% while every content-bearing category collapses** — systems know when they don't know, but not what's true now.
3. **MemoryArena:** agents near-perfect on LoCoMo drop to 40–60%; swapping active memory for long-context-only took task completion from &gt;80% to ~45%.

**GateMem's Memory Governance Score is the best top-line metric published for a system like yours:**

```
MGS = U · (1 − A) · (1 − F)
  U = utility (correct answer AND judge-confirmed factual coverage)
  A = access-control violation rate
  F = active-forgetting failure rate
```

Multiplicative on purpose: *"a system cannot obtain a high overall score merely by being highly useful if it leaks protected information, nor by being perfectly secure if it paralyzes legitimate authorized queries."* Track over-refusal rate separately.

### The metric stack to actually implement

From the 2026 survey (arXiv:2603.07670), four layers:
- **L1 task effectiveness** — success rate, factual correctness
- **L2 memory quality** — retrieved-record precision/recall, **contradiction rate**, **staleness distribution**, coverage
- **L3 efficiency** — latency p50/p95, prompt tokens, retrieval calls, storage growth
- **L4 governance** — privacy leakage, deletion compliance, access-scope violations

Plus, from practitioners:
- **Zep's Context Completeness** (COMPLETE / PARTIAL / INSUFFICIENT) as the *primary* metric, because it isolates memory quality from LLM generation quality.
- **`pass^k`, not `pass@k`.** At 75% per-trial success, pass@3 ≈ 98% but **pass^3 ≈ 42%**. For a nightly job that can silently poison the knowledge base, pass^k is the honest number.
- **Letta's Memory Write task design** — ingest a transcript, **delete the transcript**, then ask questions only answerable from extracted facts. Plus a penalty for extraneous memory operations, because "models good at archival memory might overuse memory operations when unnecessary."
- **Chroma's generative benchmarking** — build the golden set from *your* documents. Their headline: text-embedding-3-large scores 0.997 Recall@10 on clean Wikipedia and **0.552 on real production queries**, and jina-v3 beats text-embedding-3-large on *every MTEB English task* while losing on the real corpus. **Never pick an embedding model from MTEB.** Their 472-query eval set cost ~$4.72.
- **Chroma's Context Rot** (18 models, 194,480 calls): performance degrades with input length even for verbatim replication; degrades *faster* for low needle–question similarity; and across **all 18 models**, performance was better on shuffled haystacks than logically structured ones. Their LongMemEval slice: 113k-token full prompt vs ~300-token focused prompt, with the focused prompt scoring **significantly higher** — the strongest quantitative case for your architecture.
- **Krippendorff's α, not Cohen's κ**, for your review queue (reviewers dip in and out; κ can't handle incomplete data). α ≥ 0.800 reliable, 0.667–0.800 tentative. Beware the kappa paradox with a 90% accept rate.
- **ECE (15 bins) + MCE** on extractor confidence; use **MCE** for the band you auto-accept without review. Temperature scaling is the right default.

**One thing to skip:** truth-discovery algorithms (TruthFinder et al.). The QCRI experimental evaluation found that with fewer than ~8 distinct values per item, *"most of the methods perform worse than random guessing, with precision from .1 to .4"* — and MajorityVoting hits 0.9804 precision in 0.009s. A team knowledge base is exactly the low-conflict regime. What *is* worth taking is CATD's shrinkage idea: `reliability(s) = (accepted(s) + α·μ) / (total(s) + α)` with α ≈ 10–20, so a source with 2 accepted facts doesn't get reliability 1.0. Your review queue gives you `accepted`/`total` for free.

## 5. Directly applicable to your MongoDB stack

**MongoDB shipped the whole modern retrieval stack in June 2026** `[V-direct]`:

- **`$rankFusion`** — MongoDB 8.0+, GA 2026-06-30. `RRFscore(d) = Σ w/(60 + rank(d))`, **60 hardcoded**. Sub-pipelines limited to `$match`/`$search`/`$vectorSearch`/`$sample`/`$geoNear`/`$sort`/`$skip`/`$limit`. `scoreDetails: true` gives per-pipeline rank/weight breakdown. (On 8.0.X you must open a support case.)
- **`$scoreFusion` + `$score`** — 8.3+. `normalization: none|sigmoid|minMaxScaler`, `combination.method: avg|expression`. **`$score` lets you compute an arbitrary expression as `$meta:"score"`** — which is how you fold Redis's recency formula, or your `confidence` field, into ranking *inside the database* instead of re-sorting in Node. This is the single highest-leverage change available to you.
- **`$rerank`** — 8.3+, **Atlas cloud only, Preview**. Voyage `rerank-2.5` at $0.05/1M tokens, 200M free tokens per org. Overwrites `$meta:"score"`, so project the fusion score into a named field first. Errors if any `path` field is missing on any input doc.
- **Automated Embedding** (`type: "autoEmbed"`) — vectors live in an internal system collection, so **your documents stay clean** (no `embedding: number[]` bloat). Change-stream-driven sync.
- `mongodb/mongodb-atlas-local` now publishes **8.3.0** tags, so your `knowledge-test-mongo` container can exercise `$rankFusion`/`$scoreFusion`/`$score` locally — but not `$rerank`.

The canonical pipeline is now **`$rankFusion`(vector + text + `$score` recency) → `$rerank` → `$limit`**. Your current `buildFactsSearchPipeline` is BM25-only with `embedding` stubbed — that's the gap.

**One package worth reading:** `@mongodb-developer/vercel-ai-memory` (npm v0.4.2, 2026-06-25) — 2 GitHub stars but listed as a first-class memory provider in the Vercel AI SDK v7 docs. Its retention model is the most complete forgetting implementation on MongoDB: four modes including `ttl+importance` (important memories immune to TTL) and `dynamic` (per-doc `computeExpireAt` backed by an `expire_at` TTL index at `expireAfterSeconds: 0`, recomputed on read). Plus `is_latest` supersession pre-filtered *inside* `$vectorSearch.filter`.

### Gaps I found in your code

`[V-direct]` from reading `packages/knowledge/`:

1. **`validUntil` is declared but never written.** It appears only in `schemas/facts.ts` and `currentlyValidFilter`. You have transaction-time supersession without valid-time. Adding `validFrom` + writing `validUntil` gives you the Graphiti model.
2. **The erasure cascade doesn't follow `derivedFrom`.** `erasure.ts` deletes by `{"anchors.personId": personId}`; `derivedFrom` appears nowhere in that file. Combined with (3), this is exploitable.
3. **Consolidation narrows anchors.** `anchorDraftField()` returns a *single* anchor field, so merging under an org anchor drops the parents' `personId`s. Concrete leak: two facts about Anna and Petra merge into "Anna and Petra both pushed back on the Q3 timeline" anchored only to Acme; `erasePerson(Anna)` deletes the parent but the merged fact survives, still naming Anna, with a dangling `derivedFrom`. Fixes: union parent anchors into the draft; add a bounded fixpoint over `derivedFrom` (depth cap ~8, visited set); run the name-token redactor over `facts.text`; assert post-erasure referential integrity.
4. **Consolidation handles redundancy but not contradiction.** `buildConsolidationPrompt` asks only for merges that "reduce redundancy with ZERO information loss" — contradictory facts are neither redundant nor mergeable under those rules, so the dream cycle skips them. Contradiction is only handled at extraction time (`extraction.ts`: "If a fact updates or contradicts a currently valid fact, list that fact's id in `supersedes`"), which means **a contradiction between two facts extracted from different sources at different times is never detected.** That's the inter-context conflict case — your most common one.
5. **No keyed judge log.** `proposals` records `resolvedBy`/`resolvedAt` but not the model, prompt, or verdict. TOKI calls this admitting *replay inconsistency*.
6. **Your consolidator only sees `id | category | text`.** Auto-Dreamer (arXiv:2605.20616) gives its consolidator a `get_source_trace` tool so it can check the original transcript before merging. Your "ZERO information loss" instruction can only *ask* for that; a tool can enforce it.

A cheap, high-value addition for (4): NLI-based contradiction detection as a pre-filter. `cross-encoder/nli-deberta-v3-base` (SNLI 92.38, MNLI-mm 90.04) over currently-valid facts sharing the same anchor **and** category — the same scoping trick Zep's paper credits for tractability. `contradiction` → escalate to the LLM adjudicator; `entailment` → route to the merge path; `neutral` → coexist. That keeps the expensive judge off the ~95% of writes that aren't conflicts.

# 2. Execution model &amp; durability

**Three levels** (`concepts/execution-model-and-durability.mdx`): session (durable conversation, days/weeks) → turn (one user message + all work it triggers) → step (durable checkpoint = one model call + its tool calls).

**Underneath: the open-source Workflow SDK** (`workflow-sdk.dev`). Vendored `@workflow/*` at the **`5.0.0-beta` line** (`@workflow/core 5.0.0-beta.38`, `world-vercel 5.0.0-beta.34`, `world-local 5.0.0-beta.32` per `package.json`). Nitro hosts HTTP routes + workflow entrypoints but is **not** the state store.

| Host | World | Storage |
|---|---|---|
| Vercel | Vercel Workflow | platform (adds latest-prod-deployment routing, dashboard run metadata) |
| local / `eve start` | SDK local world | `.eve/.workflow-data` on disk |
| custom | `experimental.workflow.world: "@workflow/world-postgres"` | that package's env vars |

Custom worlds "must implement the runtime protocol expected by eve's vendored `@workflow/*` packages… the Workflow SDK rejects incompatible protocol versions during initialization." npm `latest` for world packages "can lag behind that line" — pin explicitly.

**Crash / timeout / redeploy** (L57-63): run resumes from the last completed step. **Completed steps never re-run** — eve replays the recorded result from its journal and emits nothing to the stream. **A step interrupted mid-execution re-runs.** Consequence stated flatly: "make non-idempotent side effects like charges or emails idempotent, or gate them with approval." Whatever the interrupted attempt already wrote to the stream **stays**, and the retry re-emits under *new* `meta.id`s with the same `turnId`/`stepIndex`/`sequence`. **No field records which attempt finished** (`concepts/sessions-runs-and-streaming.md` L123).

**Retry budgets**:
- Durable steps: "eve runs each durable step up to four times" (`sessions-runs-and-streaming.md` L123).
- Subagent model calls: "at most three fresh model-call attempts" for classified transient provider failures incl. mid-stream overload; other recoverable errors fall back to Workflow's durable step retry from the last committed snapshot (`subagents.mdx` L141).

**Parked work** (L65-67): approvals, interactive OAuth, long-running subagents ⇒ the workflow suspends and **holds no compute**. `sleep()` tool (`eve/tools/sleep`, opt-in via `agent/tools/sleep.ts`) sleeps the durable turn workflow without holding an app runtime open.

**Serverless mapping / your nightly consolidation job**: the durable turn survives a function timeout because it checkpoints at step boundaries. Two caveats that matter for you:
1. **Markdown-form schedules run in "task mode": they cannot park.** "A task-mode session runs to completion or fails, and cannot park to wait for a person or an OAuth sign-in" (`schedules.mdx` L34). Task-mode sessions also **skip** the token-limit continuation prompt and instead fail the next model call with `SESSION_TOKEN_LIMIT_REACHED` (`agent-config.md` L161-165). A consolidation job that needs a human decision must use the handler form.
2. **Handler form (`run`) can park.** `run({ receive, waitUntil, appAuth })` — `waitUntil(promise)` extends the cron invocation's lifetime; `receive(channel, {message, target, auth})` starts a normal durable session that *can* park.

**Message queueing is not a queue** (L69-77): "eve does not maintain a durable FIFO queue of user messages for a session." One active session owns a continuation token; competing input is **not forwarded to the owner**. Concurrent sends to one session are best-effort folded. Guidance: send one turn, wait for `session.waiting`, keep your own per-session queue in the channel/app layer.

# 3. Context control (the heart of it)

**What goes into context each turn** (`concepts/context-control.md`, `concepts/default-harness.md`, `instructions.mdx`):
- `instructions.md`/`.ts` — always-on system prompt, prepended to **every** model call.
- Skills — **not** in the prompt; only their *descriptions* are advertised, plus a framework-owned `load_skill` tool. On call, eve "appends the skill's markdown to the active instructions for later turn work."
- Workspace — **not inlined**. Model gets "a shallow workspace hint, a separate skill-root hint," and inspects via `bash`/`read_file`/`glob`/`grep`.
- Conversation history — append-only, summarized by compaction.

**Instructions directory composition** (`instructions.mdx` L33-37): `agent/instructions/` read **non-recursively**, accepts `.md` and `.ts` (`defineInstructions` or `defineDynamic`). Entries combine **alphabetically by filename (`localeCompare`)**. A root flat file's content comes **first**, then sorted dir entries. Authoring both `instructions.md` and `instructions.ts` at root is a **build error**.

**Critical**: `defineInstructions` in a static `.ts` **runs once at build time** and is captured into the compiled manifest — "the runtime serves the same prompt every session and never re-runs the module." Only `defineDynamic` re-resolves per session/turn.

**Compaction** (`concepts/default-harness.md` L8-25):
- Trigger: conversation tokens **plus the estimated fixed checkpoint-prompt envelope** compared against `thresholdPercent` (default `0.9`) — so it fires sooner than a conversation-only estimate.
- Summarizes older turns; prompt asks the model to separate completed progress/decisions from remaining work and retain constraints, preferences, data, references.
- On re-compaction, the **previous checkpoint is passed separately and without per-message truncation**, then replaced.
- Automatically preserves framework tool state: **resets read-before-write tracking** (a later write re-reads the file) and **re-injects the active todo list**. "There is no per-tool hook to configure."
- Image/file content parts are **dropped** on compaction and replaced with a text stub. "The model cannot re-see a compacted image" (`tools/overview.mdx` L112).
- Events: `compaction.requested` (carries `modelId, sessionId, turnId, usageInputTokens`) → `compaction.completed`.
- Manual: `ClientSession.compact()`, custom-route `compact({continuationToken})`, `POST /eve/v1/session/compact`, TUI `/compact`. Queues behind an active turn; appends no user message. If summarization fails, previous history is preserved.
- Destructive alternative: `ClientSession.clear()` / `POST /eve/v1/session/clear` → discards model-message history, **preserves** session identity, system prompt, tools/skills, durable state, limits, sandbox. Stream boundary `context.cleared` → `session.waiting`.

**Pruning**: none beyond compaction and clear. No token-budget pruning knob, no per-message eviction policy exposed.

**Can you inject retrieved facts per-turn?** Two supported paths, and one explicitly forbidden one:

- ✅ **Dynamic instructions** — `agent/instructions/&lt;file&gt;.ts` exporting `defineDynamic({events})` returning `defineInstructions({markdown})`. Resolves on `session.started` and `turn.started`. This is the sanctioned per-turn injection point.
- ✅ **A retrieval tool** the model calls (`defineTool`), which is model-initiated rather than forced.
- ❌ **Hooks cannot do it.** `guides/hooks.md` L29: "Handlers are **observe-only. They cannot inject model context.** To contribute runtime model messages, use `defineDynamic` and `defineInstructions` in `agent/instructions/`."

**Exact firing order** — two docs state it identically (`concepts/sessions-runs-and-streaming.md` L213-222 "Dispatch order"; `guides/hooks.md` L122-129):

1. **Channel adapter handler** runs; can mutate adapter state.
2. **Metadata projection** — framework re-evaluates the channel's `metadata(state)` and stores the result.
3. **Event is stamped with its `meta` envelope and written to the durable stream.** (Hooks always run *after* durable recording, "so if a hook throws, the stream stays consistent.")
4. **Hooks** fire: **typed handlers first, then the `*` wildcard**. Return values ignored.
5. **Dynamic resolvers** (tool, skill, instruction, model, subagent) fire. By this point `ctx.channel.metadata` already holds the freshly projected metadata from step 2. "The order is structural, not incidental."

Resolvers across files run **concurrently**. The tool loop reads the current tool set **right before each model call**, so a mid-turn update is visible on the next call.

**Resolver event matrix** (`guides/dynamic-capabilities.md`):

| Surface | `session.started` | `turn.started` | `step.started` | `fallback` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| model | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | required |
| tools | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | build error |
| skills | ✅ | ✅ | ❌ reserved | build error |
| instructions | ✅ | ✅ | ❌ | build error |
| subagents | ✅ | ✅ | ❌ | build error |

Within one file, "the most recently fired one owns that file's tool set." Dynamic name overrides authored name; two dynamic resolvers emitting the same name **throws**.

**Hook failure semantics** (`guides/hooks.md` L131-133): a thrown handler "propagates through the emit composer and surfaces as `turn.failed`"; if a hook on a failure-cascade event throws, it escalates to `session.failed`. **eve treats a thrown hook as a real failure** — wrap bodies in try/catch. Hooks are **at-least-once**; "no key collapses a retry."

Subagent hooks fire **only** inside the subagent scope; parent hooks do not fire for subagent turns.

# 4. State vs memory

```ts
import { defineState } from "eve/context";
const budget = defineState&lt;T&gt;("my-agent.budget", () =&gt; ({ count: 0, cap: 25 }));
// StateHandle&lt;T&gt;: get(): T   |   update(fn: (cur: T) =&gt; T): void
```

Semantics (`guides/state.md`, `guides/session-context.md`):
- Declare **once at module scope**; import the handle everywhere.
- `get()` returns `initial()` on first access within a context.
- Values survive **workflow step boundaries** — crashes, redeploys, days-long sessions.
- `get()`/`update()` **require an active eve context**; they throw at module top level, in build scripts, and on discovery-time paths. Safe inside `defineTool().execute`, hook handlers, channel event handlers, connection `auth:`/`headers:` resolvers, and after async boundaries in the same authored chain.
- **Durable by default, does not reset between turns.** Reset explicitly from a `turn.started` hook.
- **Never shared with subagents** — every child (built-in `agent` copy *or* declared specialist) starts with fresh state. "`defineState` values never cross the parent/child boundary, even when the child is a copy of the same agent."

**Why the memory pattern says not to use it for long-term memory** (`guides/state.md` L71-72 + `patterns/multi-tenant-memory.md`): `defineState` is *conversation-scoped working memory that lives and dies with the session* — "the agent's short-term memory, persisted durably for the life of the session." Anything that must (a) outlive the session, (b) be shared across sessions or users, or (c) **be queried independently of a turn** belongs in an external store. Add three more reasons that matter to you specifically:
- It is only readable inside a managed runtime scope — you cannot query it from your Next.js app or a MongoDB aggregation.
- It has no tenant dimension; it's keyed by session, and sessions are one-per-conversation.
- **eve exposes no API to delete durable session state.** `agent-config.md` L144: session expiration "does not delete stored session data." Grepping all 79 doc files turns up **no** session-delete route, no purge/erase API. For GDPR Art. 17 that means anything you put in `defineState` (or in conversation history) is outside your erasure cascade unless you own the workflow world. This is the single strongest architectural argument for keeping facts in your Mongo layer and treating eve sessions as ephemeral compute.

# 5. Tools

```ts
import { defineTool } from "eve/tools";
export default defineTool({
  description: string,                       // written for the model
  inputSchema: ZodSchema | StandardSchema | JSONSchemaObject,   // REQUIRED; z.object({}) for none
  outputSchema?: ZodSchema | StandardSchema, // optional; types the execute return
  approval?: Approval,
  execute(input, ctx): unknown | Promise&lt;unknown&gt;,   // sync or async
  toModelOutput?(output): ToolOutput,
});
```
Plain JSON Schema types `input` as `Record&lt;string, unknown&gt;`.

**`ctx`** (`tools/overview.mdx` L37-45, `reference/typescript-api.md` L61-68):
- `ctx.session` — `{ id, turn: { id, sequence }, auth: { current, initiator }, parent?: { callId, sessionId, rootSessionId, turn } }` (read-only)
- `ctx.callId` — current tool call id, carried on stream events and approval context
- `ctx.toolName` — final runtime name incl. namespace qualification
- `ctx.abortSignal` — aborts on turn cancellation; `ctx.getSandbox()` sessions already bound to it
- `ctx.getSandbox()` — async, no args, one sandbox per agent
- `ctx.getSkill(id)` — sync handle, `{ name, file(relPath) }`, lazy read from sandbox
- `ctx.getToken(provider, options?)` / `ctx.requireAuth(provider, options?)` — inline auth providers; `requireAuth` evicts the cached token and re-challenges (use after a downstream 401)

Tools run **in the app runtime** with full `process.env` and `lib/` imports — not in the sandbox. "eve never runs authored tools during discovery."

**Approval** (`tools/overview.mdx`, `connections/mcp.mdx` L146-187, `patterns/multi-tenant-approvals.md`):
- Helpers from `eve/tools/approval`: `always()`, `once()` (first time per session), `never()`.
- **Conditional: yes.** `approval` accepts a policy function, sync or async, receiving `ApprovalContext = { session, toolName, toolInput, approvedTools }` and returning `ApprovalStatus`:
  - `"user-approval"` or `true` → pause for a human
  - `"not-applicable"` or `false` → run without prompt
  - `{ type: "approved", reason? }` / `{ type: "denied", reason? }` → decide automatically
- For connection tools, `toolName` arrives **qualified** (`social__delete_draft`) — match with `.includes()`/`.endsWith()`, not `===`. `toolInput` is `Record&lt;string, unknown&gt; | undefined` from a schema **you don't own** — read defensively.
- Types `ApprovalContext`, `ApprovalStatus` import from `eve/tools`.
- Ordering: **approval runs before `execute`**; with OAuth it's approve-then-sign-in, and "eve records the approval on session state the moment it's granted, and that record survives the sign-in park" — one approval, one sign-in, never a double prompt.
- Warnings the docs repeat: approval is a **gate, not authorization** — "the executor must still derive and enforce tenancy again"; and built-in approval "is not a four-eyes workflow that proves a different person or role approved it."

**Error handling**: throw from `execute` → surfaces as a failed tool result to the model. Malformed raw tool args now "preserve the original JSON syntax error and return it to the model as a failed tool result" (CHANGELOG 0.29.2). Tool outputs must be JSON-serializable — convert `Date`, `Map`, `Set`, `NaN`, cyclic objects first.

**`toModelOutput`**: receives the full typed `execute` return, affects **only the model**. Channel handlers and hooks still see the full output on `action.result`. Shapes: `{type:"text", value}`, `{type:"json", value}`, or `toolOutput.content([toolOutputPart.text(...), toolOutputPart.file(base64, {mediaType})])`. **File payloads must be base64 strings** — raw `Uint8Array`/`Buffer` rejected (don't survive the durable JSON boundary). eve warns above 3 MiB; content-part images are persisted in session history and **re-sent on every subsequent model call**.

**Dynamic tools have a hard bundler constraint** (`guides/dynamic-capabilities.md` L96-98): `execute` **must be an inline function expression/arrow/method shorthand placed directly as the property value**. `execute: myFn` and `execute: makeFn()` "work on the first step but do not survive replay." Silent failure mode — worth a lint rule.

**Built-ins** (`concepts/default-harness.md`): `bash`, `read_file`, `write_file`, `glob`, `grep` (all → sandbox), `web_fetch`, `todo` (app runtime), `web_search` (provider-executed, no local executor), `ask_question`, `agent` (root only), `load_skill`, `connection_search`. Override by authoring the same slug (spread from `eve/tools/defaults` to keep description/schema/framework state — e.g. `todo`'s durable state key). Disable with `export default disableTool()`; an unknown slug **fails at build** rather than silently no-op'ing. Doc's own warning: "Review these built-in tools before production use. Disable, wrap, restrict, or require approval for any tool that can access the filesystem, network, shell, or sensitive data."

# 6. Subagents

Two mechanisms (`subagents.mdx`):

**(a) Built-in `agent` tool** — root session only. Input `{ message: string, outputSchema?: object }`. Child = fresh copy of the root: **inherits** instructions, tools (minus `agent`/`Workflow`), connections, auth, **and shares the root's sandbox** (its file writes are immediately visible to the root). Starts with **fresh conversation history and fresh state**. Emit multiple `agent` calls in one response ⇒ eve runs the batch concurrently. Disable via `agent/tools/agent.ts` → `disableTool()`.

**(b) Declared subagent** — `agent/subagents/&lt;id&gt;/agent.ts`, `defineAgent({ description, model, ... })`. **`description` is required**; the compiler rejects otherwise. Conditionally available via `defineDynamic` on `session.started`/`turn.started` (not `step.started`); nil result removes it from the model-visible surface. eve **always compiles** the child's filesystem manifest and injects the resolved config when it runs. Re-checks availability before starting the child → `SUBAGENT_UNAVAILABLE` on a stale call.

**Isolation** — declared subagents inherit **nothing**:

| Slot | built-in `agent` | declared subagent |
|---|---|---|
| Instructions | inherited | own, **optional** |
| Tools | inherited minus root-only | own `tools/` |
| Connections / Skills / Hooks | inherited | own |
| Sandbox | **shared with parent** | own, else framework default |
| State | fresh | fresh |
| Channels, Schedules | root-only | **not supported** |

"An absent slot falls back to the **framework default**, not to the root's version." Sharing requires duplicating markdown per `skills/` dir or sharing typed helpers via `lib/`.

**Isolated context: yes.** "Each subagent gets its own context and its own durable session… Nothing crosses the boundary implicitly." The child **never sees the parent's history** — the parent must pack everything into `message`.

**Results return** as the tool result of the lowered tool. Set `outputSchema` to run the child in **task mode** returning structured output. Parent stream carries only control-plane `subagent.called` (with `childSessionId`) and `subagent.completed`, plus proxied `input.requested` / `authorization.required` / `authorization.completed` so the root channel can prompt. Follow child progress at `GET /eve/v1/session/:childSessionId/stream`.

**Token quota**: each child gets a share of the parent's *remaining* quota at dispatch (remainder in the current window split evenly across the batch's local subagent calls); child usage counts against the parent. "A delegation tree can never outspend the budget configured at its root." A delegated task with **no inherited quota fails** rather than prompting.

**Cancellation** cascades recursively; the parent does **not** emit `subagent.completed` for cancelled work.

**Nesting**: unlimited for declared subagents ("nesting ends where the authored directory tree ends"; `limits.maxSubagentDepth` no longer exists). `Workflow` and `agent` are root-only and never recurse.

**Your two use cases:**
- **Nightly consolidator**: a subagent **cannot** be the cron entry point — `schedules/` is root-only. Shape it as root `agent/schedules/consolidate.ts` → handler-form `run()` (so it can park) → delegate to a declared `consolidator` subagent with a narrow tool surface (Mongo read/write only, no `bash`, no `web_fetch`). Use `experimental_workflow({maxSubagents})` if you need per-tenant fan-out with programmatic map-reduce (`guides/dynamic-workflows.md`) — orchestration JS runs in a **QuickJS sandbox** that can reach only `tools.&lt;name&gt;` and language built-ins (no files, network, shell, skills, connections). Note delivery is **at least once** and side effects need application-level idempotency (`patterns/dynamic-scheduling.md` L246).
- **Fact-verifier**: good fit as a declared subagent with `outputSchema` — isolated prompt, isolated tool surface, structured return, own token budget, own trace. But if verification is deterministic, a plain tool is cheaper: a subagent costs a full child session + sandbox.

Repeated warning worth heeding: "Do not rely on subagent delegation by itself as an approval boundary."

## 7.5 `eve/evals/expect` — every matcher `[dist expect/index.d.ts]`

| Builder | Signature | Scores | Default severity |
|---|---|---|---|
| `includes(substring)` | `(string \| RegExp) =&gt; Assertion` | coerced string contains substring / matches RegExp | **gate** |
| `equals(expected)` | `(unknown) =&gt; Assertion` | deep structural equality | **gate** |
| `matches(schema)` | `(StandardSchemaV1) =&gt; Assertion` | validates against Standard Schema (e.g. Zod) | **gate** |

## 7.6 Matcher mini-language `[dist match.d.ts]`

A matcher value is one of:
- **literal** — objects **partial-deep-match** (every key in the matcher must match recursively; nested values are themselves matchers); arrays match **element-wise**; primitives compare with `Object.is`
- **RegExp** — tests string values directly, and the **JSON serialization** of anything else. eve resets `lastIndex` (`testRegExp`) so `g`/`y` flags don't produce order-dependent results
- **function** `(value) =&gt; boolean`

`EveEvalCountMatcher = number | ((count: number) =&gt; boolean)`. A number is an **exact** count of calls matching every supplied constraint; default is "at least one".

```ts
EveEvalToolCallMatchOptions      = { input?, output?, status?, count? }
EveEvalSkillLoadMatchOptions     = Omit&lt;ToolCallMatchOptions, "input"&gt;
EveEvalSubagentCallMatchOptions  = { callId?, childSessionId?, remoteUrl?, output?, status?, count? }
EveEvalInputRequestMatchOptions  = { display?, input?, optionIds?, prompt?, toolName? }   // toolName is a plain string
EveEvalEventMatch&lt;TType&gt;         = { type: TType, data?: &lt;partial-deep over that event's data&gt;, count? }
EveEvalActionStatus              = "pending" | "completed" | "failed" | "rejected"        // default "completed"
```

`optionIds` receives ids **in request order**; a literal array must match the complete ordered list exactly.

```ts
t.calledTool("bash", { input: { command: /^pwd/ }, count: 1 });
t.calledTool("echo", { count: (c) =&gt; c &gt;= 2 });
t.calledTool("echo", { output: (v) =&gt; String(v).includes(marker) });
parked.calledTool("guarded", { status: "pending", count: 1 });
t.calledSubagent("weather", { remoteUrl: (v) =&gt; v === process.env.WEATHER_AGENT_URL, output: /72F/ });
turn.eventOrder([
  { type: "subagent.called",    data: { name: "researcher" },          count: 2 },
  { type: "subagent.completed", data: { subagentName: "researcher" },  count: 2 },
]);
```

**Derived facts** the runner extracts (`EveEvalDerivedFacts`): `toolCalls[]` (`{name, input, output, status, turnIndex, sessionId?}`), `toolCallCount`, `subagentCalls[]` (`{callId?, childSessionId?, name, remoteUrl?, output?, status, turnIndex, sessionId?}`), `subagentCallCount`, `inputRequests[]`, `parked: boolean`, `messageCount`, `reasoningBlockCount`, `failureCode?`.

## 7.7 Severity &amp; verdicts

`.gate(threshold?)` hard · `.soft(threshold?)` tracked (soft with **no threshold = tracked-only, never fails**) · `.atLeast(threshold)` ≡ `.soft(threshold)` · `.label(name)` stable name for repeated families.

Defaults: **all scoped/run-level methods are gates**; `includes`/`equals`/`matches`/`satisfies` are gates; `similarity` and **every `t.judge.*`** are soft.

`await t.require(value, assertion)` records a gate, **returns the original value** on pass, and **stops the test body** on fail without adding a duplicate execution error. `turn.requireToolCall(name, opts?)` and `session.requireInputRequest(filter?)` are the protocol-data equivalents. Recorded assertions never throw and are **not awaitable**.

Verdicts `[dist types.d.ts:460]`: `"passed"` (no execution error, every gate held, every soft threshold met) · `"failed"` (gate failed or execution errored) · `"scored"` (gates held, a soft assertion fell below threshold) · `"skipped"`.

## 7.8 Judge

`t.judge.autoevals.*` — eve-owned wrappers over **Braintrust autoevals** (dep `autoevals@0.0.132`). Complete list `[dist types.d.ts:303-308]`:

| Grader | Signature | Grades |
|---|---|---|
| `factuality(expected, opts?)` | `(string, JudgeOpts?)` | factual consistency of the reply against an expected answer, **A–E buckets** |
| `summarizes(expected, opts?)` | `(string, JudgeOpts?)` | how well the reply summarizes the expected text |
| `closedQA(criteria, opts?)` | `(string, JudgeOpts?)` | whether the reply satisfies a free-form **yes/no** criterion (no expected answer) |
| `sql(expected, opts?)` | `(string, JudgeOpts?)` | **semantic equivalence** of two SQL statements |

`JudgeOpts = { on?: unknown, model?: LanguageModel, modelOptions?: AgentModelOptionsDefinition }`. `on` defaults to `t.reply`.

Judge model resolution, **innermost wins**: per-call `opts.model` → per-eval `defineEval({judge})` → `defineEvalConfig({judge})`. Resolved **once when the runner builds `t`**; **never** the model under test.

- String id ⇒ Vercel AI Gateway, needs `AI_GATEWAY_API_KEY` or `VERCEL_OIDC_TOKEN`. `LanguageModel` instance ⇒ used directly.
- **Model configured but no credentials ⇒ the eval skips visibly**, not fails.
- **No judge model resolved at all + a `t.judge.*` call ⇒ recorded as a failed gate** (the runner scores after the test body, the missing model throws).
- Judge calls **start when recorded**; the runner awaits all of them during finalization.

Failure output includes prompt, criteria, response, rationale, choice.

## 7.9 `mockModel` `[dist mock-model.d.ts]`

```ts
import { mockModel } from "eve/evals";
mockModel(input?: MockModelOptions | MockModelResponder | string): LanguageModel
```
- `mockModel()` → `"Mock response"`. `mockModel("text")` → static.
- Callback `MockModelResponder = (req: MockModelRequest) =&gt; MockModelResponse | string | Promise&lt;...&gt;`
- `MockModelRequest = { messages: {role, text}[], userMessages: string[], lastUserMessage: string|null, userMessageCount: number, tools: {name, description?, inputSchema?}[], toolResults: {id, name, output, isError}[] }`
- `MockModelResponse = { text?, toolCalls?: {name, input?, id?}[], usage?: {inputTokens?, outputTokens?} }`
- Options form: `{ modelId?: string /* default "model" */, provider?: string /* default "eve-mock" */, respond? }`
- Handles both generated and streamed responses, derives deterministic response metadata, estimates token usage when `usage` omitted.
- **Caveat**: it goes in `agent/agent.ts` — it is part of the *agent definition*, so use it for a **dedicated fixture agent**. It stays mocked whether that fixture runs locally or as a deployed target. You cannot mock the model of your real agent from inside an eval file.

## 7.10 Targets

`EveEvalTarget = { kind: "local" | "remote", url: string, capabilities: { devRoutes: boolean } }`. Target is **always an HTTP URL**. Runner polls `/eve/v1/health`, verifies `/eve/v1/info`, exposes `t.target`.

`EveEvalTargetHandle` methods:
- `fetch(path, init?)` → authenticated fetch (channel/webhook ingress)
- `dispatchSchedule(id)` → `{ scheduleId, sessionIds }`. **Dev-routes only** — the local `eve eval` server or a deployment in development mode; **throws** otherwise
- `attachSession(sessionId, { startIndex? })` → `Promise&lt;EveEvalSession&gt;`; consumes **one turn** from an externally-created session so its events feed run-level assertions. On a `session.waiting` boundary it recovers the continuation token, so `.send()`/`.respond()` continue the same durable session
- `watchTurn(sessionId, { startIndex? })` → `EveEvalLiveTurn` (**sync**), starts consuming immediately

**Auth** (`evals/targets.mdx`): local sends none. For `--url`, eve reads `VERCEL_ORG_ID`+`VERCEL_PROJECT_ID` (or `.vercel/project.json`), asks Vercel to resolve the exact HTTPS origin, and sends ambient credentials **only when the project IDs match** — an arbitrary URL stays anonymous. Then sends the resolved OIDC token as bearer + Vercel trusted-IDP header, plus `VERCEL_AUTOMATION_BYPASS_SECRET` if set. `EVE_EVAL_AUTH_TOKEN` is the explicit bearer override for non-Vercel auth. Credential-bearing clients **do not follow redirects**.

## 7.11 Reporters

`eve/evals/reporters` exports `Braintrust`, `JUnit`, and the `EvalReporter` interface.

```ts
interface EvalReporter {
  onRunStart(evaluations: readonly EveEval[], target: EveEvalTarget): void | Promise&lt;void&gt;;
  onEvalComplete(result: EveEvalResult): void | Promise&lt;void&gt;;
  onRunComplete(summary: EveEvalRunSummary): void | Promise&lt;void&gt;;
}
```
- **`Braintrust({ projectName?, experimentName?, /* base experiment by name or id */ })`**. Requires `npm install braintrust` + `BRAINTRUST_API_KEY`. Gate assertions log as **binary scores under a `gate:` prefix** so experiments diff gate regressions like soft scores. Repeated names get `#2`, `#3` — use `.label()`. Failed assertion details land in metadata under **`eveFailedAssertions`**. A reporter **instance** observes the evals that reference it; share one instance to land in one experiment. Listing a config reporter on an eval too does **not** double-report.
- **`JUnit({ filePath })`** or `--junit &lt;path&gt;` (preferred: CI owns the path). One `&lt;testcase&gt;` per path-derived id; failed gates and execution errors → failures; `t.skip(reason)` → `&lt;skipped&gt;`.
- Config reporters observe **every** eval; per-eval `reporters` scope to that eval. `--skip-report` suppresses **both**.

`EveEvalResult = { id, result: EveEvalTaskResult, assertions: AssertionResult[] /* in record order */, verdict, error?, skipReason?, startedAt, completedAt }`. `EveEvalRunSummary = { target, results, startedAt, completedAt, passed, failed, scored, skipped, errored }` — `errored` is the execution-error **subset of** `failed`.

## 7.12 CLI &amp; CI

```bash
eve eval [evalId...] [--url &lt;url&gt;] [--tag &lt;t...&gt;] [--exclude-tag &lt;t...&gt;] [--strict]
         [--timeout &lt;ms&gt;] [--max-concurrency &lt;n&gt;] [--junit &lt;path&gt;] [--list]
         [--verbose] [--json] [--skip-report]
```
Positional ids match **exactly or by directory prefix**. Tag filters compose: `--tag` keeps evals with ≥1 listed tag, then `--exclude-tag` drops. **A `--tag` that matches nothing is exit 2**; an `--exclude-tag` that removes everything is **exit 0** with nothing executed.

**Exit codes**: `0` all non-skipped passed gates (and soft thresholds under `--strict`) · `1` any failure (gate, execution error, strict threshold miss) · `2` configuration error. `t.skip()` never changes the exit code.

**Artifacts**: `.eve/evals/&lt;timestamp&gt;/` — `summary.json`, `results.jsonl` index, and per-eval assertion results, verdicts, **captured event streams**, and `t.log` lines under `evals/`.

**CI**: `eve eval --strict --junit .eve/junit.xml` (add `--url "$DEPLOY_URL"`). Upload `.eve/evals/` as a failure artifact. **Evals run against a live model** — CI must have provider credentials.

# 8. Multi-tenant auth &amp; approvals

**How a tenant id gets onto `ctx.session.auth`** (`patterns/multi-tenant-auth.md`, `guides/auth-and-route-protection.md`): you write an `AuthFn` in `agent/channels/eve.ts` that verifies your app's credential and returns a `SessionAuthContext`:

```ts
{
  authenticator: "app",
  issuer?: "https://app.example.com",   // include when ids come from &gt;1 identity system
  principalId: caller.userId,            // keep STABLE for the same user
  principalType: "user",
  subject?: caller.userId,
  attributes: { tenantId: caller.tenantId, roles: caller.roles },
}
```
"The tenant comes from verified route auth, never a prompt, tool argument, or remote API response." **There is no framework-native tenant object** — "the implementation is the composition of route auth, `ctx.session`, tool execution, and async connection auth/header resolvers."

**Direct mapping to your stack**: `appSession()` reads the better-auth session, sets `principalId = user.id`, `attributes.tenantId = activeOrganizationId`. "If one user can switch between orgs, authenticate the selected org on **every** session create or continue request and stamp that selected `tenantId` onto the current turn" — matches the better-auth active-org model exactly.

**Route protection**: an **ordered walk** on `eveChannel({ auth: [...] })` guarding `POST /eve/v1/session`, `POST /eve/v1/session/:sessionId`, `GET /eve/v1/session/:sessionId/stream`. `GET /eve/v1/health` is **always public**. Each entry: return `SessionAuthContext` (accept, stop) · return `null`/`undefined` (skip) · **throw** (`UnauthenticatedError` 401, `ForbiddenError` 403). All-skip ⇒ 401 with a `WWW-Authenticate` header derived from the configured entries' schemes. Fails closed: production browser traffic rejected unless configured; anonymous requires explicit `none()`.

Helpers from `eve/channels/auth`: `localDev()` (loopback-hostname only; **trusts the advertised Host header** — "never run on `localDev()` alone"), `vercelOidc({subjects})` + `vercelSubject({teamSlug, projectName, environment})`, `none()`, `httpBasic(creds, {realm})`, `jwtHmac()`, `jwtEcdsa()`, `oidc()`, `placeholderAuth()` (scaffolded, returns a structured 401 in production — **replace before launch**). Low-level verifiers: `verifyHttpBasic`, `verifyJwtHmac`, `verifyJwtEcdsa`, `verifyOidc`, `verifyVercelOidc`; helpers `extractBearerToken`, `withAuthChallenges(fn, challenges)`, `routeAuth(request, auth)`, `createUnauthorizedResponse({status, code, message, challenges})`, `createIpAllowList(...)`, `isIpAllowed(...)`.

**The load-bearing warning for you**, stated twice: **"Route auth does not enforce session ownership."** "There's no second per-session ownership ACL stacked on top of route auth." And in `patterns/multi-tenant-approvals.md`: you must protect `POST /eve/v1/session/:sessionId` (including `inputResponses`) and `GET /eve/v1/session/:sessionId/stream` yourself, persisting session→tenant ownership in your own DB and checking it before proxying. **`sessionId` is a bearer capability by default.**

`ctx.session.auth.current` = caller on the active inbound turn; `.initiator` = caller that started the session. A follow-up updates `current` but leaves `initiator` alone — so **a different caller can follow up on the same session** and `current` tracks them. Both are `null` only on internal runtime paths. Top-level schedule sessions get `principalId: "eve:app"`, `principalType: "runtime"`.

**Forwarded identity**: rejected with 403 by default. Requires `trustedForwarders: (forwarder) =&gt; boolean` on your authored `eveChannel` (the framework default channel never accepts one). The predicate authorizes **the forwarder, not the forwarded principal** — `() =&gt; true` lets any route-auth-passing caller assert any principal, "including preview deployments of your own project when `vercelOidc()` is in the walk."

**Multi-tenant approvals** (`patterns/multi-tenant-approvals.md`): one async adapter reused by tools + both connection protocols.

```ts
export async function decideTenantApproval(surface, ctx: ApprovalContext): Promise&lt;ApprovalStatus&gt; {
  const current = ctx.session.auth.current;
  const tenantId = tenantIdOf(current), initiatorTenantId = tenantIdOf(ctx.session.auth.initiator);
  if (current?.principalType !== "user" || !tenantId || tenantId !== initiatorTenantId)
    return { type: "denied", reason: "The session is not pinned to one tenant user." };
  const input = ctx.toolInput as Record&lt;string, unknown&gt; | undefined;
  if (typeof input?.tenantId === "string" &amp;&amp; input.tenantId !== tenantId)
    return { type: "denied", reason: "Tool input cannot select another tenant." };
  // → your policy store
}
```
Notes: `ctx.toolName` is bare for authored tools (`transfer_funds`), qualified for connections (`billing__updateSubscription`). "The callback deliberately does not treat `approvedTools` as a session-wide grant. Every call is evaluated." Approval "is a gate, not authorization" — re-derive tenancy in the executor. Use an application idempotency key (`${ctx.session.id}:${ctx.session.turn.id}` in the doc's example). "Policy lookup failures should throw or deny, never silently allow. Recheck authorization inside side-effecting executors because membership or policy can change **while a run is parked**." And: built-in approval is not four-eyes.

Answering an approval by text works: a matching reply like `approve`/`deny` answers it; unrelated follow-up text is **held** so it does not implicitly deny. A **stale** approval never authorizes the earlier call — it's delivered to the model as a plain user message.

# 9. Deployment (and region pinning)

**Vercel** (`guides/deployment/vercel.mdx`): `eve link` → `eve deploy` (= install deps, `vercel deploy --prod`, pull env). Hosted builds set `VERCEL`, so `eve build` writes `.vercel/output`. Vercel configures: **Web runtime** (health/session/stream/channel/callback/schedule routes) · **Vercel Workflow** (durable runs, "optimistic replay preconditions enabled so stale event-log snapshots reload before they can commit") · **Vercel Cron** (schedules; cron evaluated in **UTC**) · **Vercel Sandbox**. During a Vercel build eve **automatically creates or reuses a sandbox template** when your sandbox has `bootstrap()` or seed files — "the build needs permission to create Vercel Sandbox templates, and a **prewarm failure stops the deployment**" (`--skip-sandbox-prewarm` opts out, "the output might not be deployable"). Agent Runs tab is **gated per team**.

**Self-hosting** (`guides/deployment/self-hosting.md`): `eve build` → `.output/` Nitro server → `PORT=3000 eve start --host 0.0.0.0`. You must forward **both** prefixes through your proxy, unrewritten: `/eve/` **and** `/.well-known/workflow/`. "A proxy restricted to `/eve/` lets a session start, but the run stalls when its callback can't reach eve." Workflow state defaults to `.eve/.workflow-data` — mount it on persistent storage. Or select a world package. Schedules: `eve build &amp;&amp; eve start` starts Nitro's schedule runner; **a custom HTTP-only host or preset silently does not fire schedules** — "the schedule definitions still compile, but they will not fire automatically."

## Region pinning — the critical finding

**The eve docs never mention regions, data residency, or the EU.** I grepped all 79 doc files for `region|residenc|GDPR|frankfurt|fra1|iad1|data.transfer`. **Zero hits.** There is no `region` option on `defineAgent`, `defineSandbox`, any backend factory, `eveChannel`, or anything else in the documented surface.

**What eve pulls toward Vercel-managed components by default:**

| Component | Default | EU-residency escape hatch |
|---|---|---|
| Model routing | String model id ⇒ **Vercel AI Gateway**. Docs say only: "Model use is subject to the terms, data-processing commitments, **retention behavior**, and available controls of the selected provider and routing path" (`agent-config.md` L44) | Pass a provider-authored `LanguageModel` (`@ai-sdk/anthropic` etc.) — bypasses the gateway entirely, you own the provider contract and endpoint |
| Durable state (full conversation + tool I/O) | Vercel Workflow when `VERCEL` is set | `experimental.workflow.world: "@workflow/world-postgres"` on a self-hosted Node service — **but flagged as "opt-in settings that can change or disappear in any release. Treat them as unstable."** |
| Sandbox | `defaultBackend()` picks **Vercel Sandbox** whenever `process.env.VERCEL` is set — "since local container/VM runtimes can't run there" | `docker()`, `microsandbox()`, or a custom `SandboxBackend` — **only viable off-Vercel** |
| Cron | Vercel Cron | Nitro schedule runner via `eve start`, or your own scheduler hitting an authenticated route |
| Route auth | `vercelOidc()` in the framework default | Any custom `AuthFn`; "Vercel OIDC is optional" |
| Connection OAuth | Vercel Connect (optional) | `defineInteractiveAuthorization` — self-hosted OAuth, three methods, no Vercel Connect |
| Eval reporting | Braintrust (US SaaS) | `JUnit({filePath})` or a custom `EvalReporter` |
| Telemetry | zero-config local spool, or your OTel exporter | fully yours |

**Practical read**: on Vercel you get **no documented control over where durable session state, sandbox compute, or gateway inference physically run.** If EU residency is a hard requirement, the only shape the docs support is **self-hosted Nitro in an EU region + `@workflow/world-postgres` (unstable flag) on EU Postgres + `docker()`/`microsandbox()` sandbox + direct EU provider endpoints + no Braintrust.** That strips away most of what makes eve attractive on your existing Vercel stack, and puts you on an explicitly-unstable config surface. This is the single biggest architectural risk in your evaluation and it is a docs gap, not just a config gap — you would need to confirm with Vercel directly.

`agent/instrumentation.ts`, **root-only**, auto-discovered, "runs at server startup before any agent code. Its presence implicitly enables telemetry, so there is no separate `isEnabled` toggle."

```ts
export default defineInstrumentation({
  setup: ({ agentName }) =&gt; registerOTel({ serviceName: agentName, traceExporter: /* ... */ }),
  recordInputs?: boolean,        // default TRUE — full message history on each step span
  recordOutputs?: boolean,       // default TRUE — model outputs on spans
  functionId?: string,
  traceChannelRequests?: boolean,// default false
```

**Three surfaces**:
1. **Workflow run tags `$eve.*`** — automatic, framework-owned, **not configurable**, on the Vercel Workflow run (not on OTel spans). Structural: `$eve.type` (`"session"|"turn"|"subagent"`), `$eve.parent`, `$eve.root`, `$eve.subagent`, `$eve.trigger`, `$eve.title` (**truncated title derived from the first user message** — note that's user content leaving into run metadata). 2. **OpenTelemetry export** — any OTel backend (Braintrust, Raindrop, Arize, Honeycomb, Datadog, Jaeger). 3. **Runtime context events** — `events["step.started"](input)` returns `{ runtimeContext: Record&lt;string,string&gt; }` which rides onto the model-call span and children. `input` = `{ session, turn, step, channel, modelInput }`. Channel kind: `channel:&lt;name&gt;` for authored, `http`/`schedule`/`subagent` for framework, `unknown` otherwise. Narrow with `isChannel(input.channel, myChannel)`.

**Zero-config local traces** (only when no `instrumentation.ts` exists): `.eve/traces/v1`, immutable OTLP/JSON spool, one trace per session. Model/tool spans carry **system prompt, prompt messages, response text, call args and results, capped at 32 KB** — `EVE_TRACES_CONTENT=off` to suppress. Gateway-served calls also carry `gen_ai.usage.cost`, `.gateway_cost`, `.input_cost`, `.output_cost`, `gen_ai.generation.id`; plus `gen_ai.usage.cache_read.input_tokens` / `.cache_creation.input_tokens`. **GDPR-relevant defaults** stated explicitly in the doc: "**By default, telemetry records full message history and model outputs.** You may need to disclose these data flows in your privacy materials." And: "For sensitive, regulated, or production data, set `recordInputs` and `recordOutputs` to `false` unless you have reviewed the exporter and its data-retention path." For your product these should be `false` from day one.

**Debugging**: `eve info [--json]`; artifacts under `.eve/discovery/agent-discovery-manifest.json`, `.eve/discovery/diagnostics.json`, `.eve/compile/compiled-agent-manifest.json`, `.eve/compile/compile-metadata.json`, `.eve/compile/module-map.mjs`. `eve logs` (JSONL), `eve logs --dump`, `eve logs --events` (interleaves session events resolved from `.eve/.workflow-data`).

# 11. Honest assessment

**The self-description.** `README.md` L15: "eve is in preview; the framework, APIs, documentation, and behavior may change before general availability." Version `0.29.5`, **88 releases** in the CHANGELOG, still `0.x`.

**What you'd be betting on — the dependency stack is pre-1.0 all the way down:**
- `@workflow/core@5.0.0-beta.38`, `@workflow/world-vercel@5.0.0-beta.34`, `world-local@5.0.0-beta.32` — the **durability substrate is a beta**, and eve *vendors* it. Custom worlds "must implement the runtime protocol expected by eve's vendored `@workflow/*` packages… the Workflow SDK rejects incompatible protocol versions during initialization." You are coupled to a beta protocol version.
- `next@16.3.0-preview.6`, `ai@^7.0.38`, `zod@4.4.3`, `vitest@4.1.10`.
- `engines: { node: "&gt;=24" }`. Your monorepo is Bun 1.1.43 / Turborepo — eve's CLI adapts to the launching package manager, but the runtime floor is Node 24.

**Release velocity is high and minor bumps carry removals.** 0.29.0 removed `/channels`, `eve channels add`, and the programmatic onboarding API, and renamed `eve trace` → `eve traces`. 0.29.5 renamed the TUI `/new` → `/reset`. A prior release made `SandboxBackendHandle.shutdown()` required and removed `dispose()` — "Breaking change for custom sandbox backends." At ~5 releases per minor across 88 releases, expect churn on a quarterly cadence.

**Specific sharp edges:**

1. **No data-deletion API.** No documented route, helper, or config for deleting durable session state. `agent-config.md` L144: session expiration "does not delete stored session data." For GDPR Art. 17 you'd have to reach into the workflow world's storage yourself — which means self-hosting a world you control. On Vercel Workflow you have no documented deletion path at all.
2. **No region control.** Covered above. Zero doc coverage.
3. **At-least-once everywhere, with an explicit "no key collapses a retry."** Interrupted steps re-run; hooks re-fire; retried steps re-emit under new `meta.id`s with **identical** `turnId`/`stepIndex`/`sequence` and no field marking which attempt won. Consequence for you: **every fact write, every proposal insert, every erasure operation must be idempotent at the Mongo layer** — that's not optional, it's the framework's stated contract. And "durable history keeps only the attempt that completed, while the event stream keeps every attempt" — so if you persist events for audit, an interrupted turn leaves both attempts in your table by design.
4. **Sessions have no queue.** "eve does not maintain a durable FIFO queue of user messages." Bursty channel ingest (your inbound email/webhook path) needs your own per-session queue. The docs say so directly.
5. **`localDev()` trusts the `Host` header.** Documented as spoofable without a normalizing proxy in front. Never ship it as the only authenticator.
6. **Route auth ≠ session ownership.** Stated twice. `sessionId` is a bearer capability. For a multi-tenant German product this is the most likely security hole — you must own that ACL.
7. **Dynamic tools' `execute` must be a literal inline function.** `execute: myFn` compiles, runs once, then silently breaks on replay. No build-time check documented.
8. **Module-backed instructions bake at build time.** Anyone reaching for `instructions.ts` expecting per-session logic gets a frozen prompt. Only `defineDynamic` re-resolves.
9. **Hooks can't inject context** and **hooks that throw fail the turn** (escalating to `session.failed` on cascade events). Both are surprising given the "observe-only" framing.
10. **Subagents inherit nothing** — every declared child duplicates its skills/tools/sandbox, or you factor into `lib/`. This gets expensive fast with many specialists.
11. **`experimental` is explicitly disposable**: "Opt-in settings that can change or disappear in any release. Treat them as unstable." That flag covers the *one* mechanism (`workflow.world`) you'd need for EU residency.
12. **`Workflow` tool runs model-authored JavaScript.** QuickJS-sandboxed with a genuine allowlist (only `tools.&lt;name&gt;` + language built-ins, no `process`/`globalThis`/`import`), but it is still the model writing and executing code. Root-only, `maxSubagents` default 100.
13. **Schedules silently don't fire on custom hosts.** Definitions compile; nothing runs. Also: `eve dev` never fires schedules on cadence — only the dev dispatch route (`POST /eve/v1/dev/schedules/:id`, never mounted in production, **no auth** because it's local-only).
14. **Telemetry defaults capture everything.** `recordInputs`/`recordOutputs` default `true`; `$eve.title` puts a truncated first user message into run metadata regardless of instrumentation config; local traces capture prompts and tool I/O by default.
15. **Evals require a live model.** No documented way to mock the model of the agent under test from inside an eval — `mockModel` lives in `agent.ts`, so it's a fixture-agent tool. Every CI eval run costs real inference against real endpoints.
16. **Agent Runs observability is gated per Vercel team** — "Contact your Vercel representative."
17. **Docs hedge in a few places worth noting**: message-drain from a session's hook is "best-effort and depends on workflow and transport timing"; `meta.id` is "time-ordered, not a total order… do not use `where id &gt; $cursor` as a lossless cursor"; sandbox `agent.session`/`agent.turn` spans "are recorded as zero-duration markers" so third-party OTel backends report zero.

**Where eve genuinely fits your product:** the eval framework is the standout — it is unusually complete for a 0.x framework (typed event matchers, deterministic + judge assertions, dataset fan-out, live-turn coordination, remote targets, JUnit/Braintrust, real exit codes). The multi-tenant auth/approval/credential patterns map cleanly onto better-auth + active-org-as-tenantId with no framework fighting. The human-review-queue shape is exactly what `approval` policies + `input.requested` are built for. Durable parked approvals that survive redeploys are hard to build yourself.

**Where it fights you:** EU residency and Art. 17 erasure are the two requirements the framework has no answer for, and they are the two you cannot compromise on. Both push you off managed Vercel onto a self-hosted Nitro service with an explicitly-unstable workflow world — at which point you've given up Vercel Workflow, Vercel Sandbox, Vercel Cron, and the Agent Runs dashboard, i.e. most of eve's operational value, while keeping all of its 0.x churn risk.

**A defensible middle path**, if you want the harness without the residency bet: use eve as the *orchestration and eval* layer with `defineState` unused, all facts/proposals/PII living exclusively in your EU MongoDB behind authored tools, sessions treated as short-lived and disposable (`limits.sessionTimeoutMs` short, `recordInputs/recordOutputs: false`, `EVE_TRACES_CONTENT=off`), and a direct EU provider endpoint instead of the gateway. That confines eve's durable state to conversation transcripts you can treat as transient — but you still need a written answer for "what's in Vercel Workflow's store, where does it live, and how do I delete it," and the docs do not give you one.</result>

### Citations and provenance — the honest answer

**`Sources`** — 4 exports: `Sources`, `SourcesTrigger` (takes `count`), `SourcesContent`, `Source` (an `&lt;a&gt;` with `href`/`title`). It's a collapsible footer list. The canonical example filters `message.parts.filter(p =&gt; p.type === "source-url")`. That's it — it's URL-shaped and coarse-grained.

**`InlineCitation`** — 15 exports: `InlineCitation`, `InlineCitationText`, `InlineCitationCard`, `InlineCitationCardTrigger` (prop `sources: string[]`, badge shows hostname + count), `InlineCitationCardBody`, `InlineCitationCarousel{,Content,Item,Header,Index,Prev,Next}`, `InlineCitationSource` (`title`, `url`, `description`), `InlineCitationQuote` (blockquote excerpt). Hover-card + carousel over multiple sources per marker.

**The load-bearing caveat, quoted verbatim from the docs:**

&gt; "Currently, there is no official support for inline citations with Streamdown or the Response component. This is because:
&gt; - There isn't any good markdown syntax for inline citations
&gt; - Language models don't naturally respond with inline citation syntax
&gt; - The AI SDK doesn't have built-in support for inline citations"

The docs' recommended workaround is `experimental_useObject` / `streamObject` with a Zod schema (`content: string` + `citations: [{number, title, url, description?, quote?}]`), then splitting on `/(\[\d+\])/` and mapping markers to `&lt;InlineCitation&gt;`. That's structured-output, not text streaming — you lose token-by-token markdown streaming.

**The better path they hint at but don't document — and I verified it works.** Streamdown v2 (which `MessageResponse` wraps) supports custom tags:

```tsx
&lt;Streamdown
  allowedTags={{ source: ["id"] }}
  components={{ source: ({ id, children }) =&gt; &lt;YourCitation factId={id}&gt;{children}&lt;/YourCitation&gt; }}
  literalTagContent={["mention"]}
&gt;
```

Without `allowedTags`, custom tags are stripped by sanitization; only listed attributes survive (`"data*"` allows all `data-*`). So for your "every claim cites the fact it came from": system-prompt the model to emit `&lt;fact id="..."&gt;…&lt;/fact&gt;`, allowlist it, and map it onto `InlineCitation` — you keep true streaming *and* get per-claim provenance. This is the approach I'd recommend. (Docs: [streamdown.ai/docs/components](https://streamdown.ai/docs/components))

Other provenance-adjacent components: `chain-of-thought` has `ChainOfThoughtSearchResults`/`SearchResult` badges for showing what was retrieved; `tool` shows raw tool I/O; `context` shows token/cost accounting.

## Section 2 — EU-resident retrieval on MongoDB Atlas

### The short version

Your instinct was right on `$rankFusion`/`$scoreFusion`/`$score`, and **the problem is worse than you thought: `autoEmbed` is disqualified for exactly the same reason as `$rerank`.** Same US inference platform, confirmed verbatim in two separate places plus MongoDB's subprocessor list.

### 1. `$rankFusion`, `$scoreFusion`, `$score` — CLEAN (VERIFIED)

All three are plain server aggregation stages. They execute inside `mongod`/`mongot` on your own cluster nodes, in your cluster's region. No model, no API key, no project-level enablement toggle, no usage-based model billing, no residency disclaimer — in sharp contrast to `$rerank` and `autoEmbed`, which have all five.

- `$rankFusion`: "first executes all input pipelines independently and then de-duplicates and combines the input pipeline results into a final ranked results set… This stage uses the Reciprocal Rank Fusion algorithm." Formula `Σ w/(60 + r(d))`. **"`$rankFusion` is only available for deployments that use MongoDB 8.0 and later."** ([docs](https://www.mongodb.com/docs/manual/reference/operator/aggregation/rankFusion/))
- `$scoreFusion`: "de-duplicates and combines the input pipeline results into a final scored results set." `combination.method` = `avg` | `expression`; `normalization` = `none|sigmoid|minMaxScaler`. **"`$scoreFusion` is only available for deployments that use MongoDB 8.3+."** ([docs](https://www.mongodb.com/docs/manual/reference/operator/aggregation/scoreFusion/))
- `$score`: "computes and returns a new score as metadata… optionally normalizes." **New in MongoDB 8.2.** ([docs](https://www.mongodb.com/docs/manual/reference/operator/aggregation/score/))

Status: **VERIFIED clean** by direct doc reading plus absence of any inference/subprocessor mechanism. Strictly, MongoDB never writes "this runs in your region" — that's inferred, but it's inferred from the fact that these are aggregation stages with no model plane at all.

### 2. `$vectorSearch` — CLEAN (VERIFIED)

Runs on the `mongot` process on your cluster's own nodes — either colocated with `mongod` or on dedicated Search Nodes. HNSW for ANN, or ENN for exhaustive. From the deployment-options doc: testing/prototyping = "MongoDB and Search processes run on the same node"; production = "MongoDB and Search processes run on different nodes." No external calls when you supply your own vectors.

Caveat: this is only true if you supply vectors yourself. The moment the index field is `type: "autoEmbed"`, see below.

### 3. `autoEmbed` — DISQUALIFIED (VERIFIED, verbatim)

This is the crux, and the answer is no. Three independent primary sources:

**(a)** [Automated Embedding Architecture](https://www.mongodb.com/docs/vector-search/crud-embeddings/automated-embedding/overview):
&gt; "**Hosted Service**: All embedding models are hosted and maintained by MongoDB. The model inference platform runs on MongoDB's infrastructure in Google Cloud cloud in a US region. You don't need to deploy, configure, or manage any model infrastructure."

**(b)** [Vector Search index reference](https://www.mongodb.com/docs/vector-search/index/vector-search-type) — the sharpest statement:
&gt; "The embedding model inference runs on a multi-tenant service in the Atlas Data Plane, available in MongoDB's Google Cloud based infrastructure in a US region. **This means that your data is sent to MongoDB's inference infrastructure for embedding generation and retrieval, regardless of your cluster's cloud provider.** Data transfer costs apply."

Note "and retrieval" — this hits **query text at read time**, not just ingest. Every user question leaves the EU.

**(c)** [MongoDB Subprocessors](https://www.mongodb.com/products/platform/trust/subprocessors) — "Customer Personal Data may be processed by the following subprocessors if you choose to utilize certain optional AI features of the Cloud Services":

| Entity | Processing | Location |
|---|---|---|
| Google LLC | Model hosting services for the optional embedding and reranking model services | **United States** |
| Baseten Labs, Inc. | Inference services for the optional embedding and reranking model services | **United States** |
| Microsoft Corp (Azure OpenAI) | AI models for certain optional generative AI features | **United States** |

Models offered: `voyage-4-lite` ($0.02/M), `voyage-4` ($0.06/M, recommended), `voyage-4-large` ($0.12/M), `voyage-code-3` ($0.18/M). autoEmbed dims are constrained to **256 | 512 | 1024 | 2048** (default 1024) and `path`/`model`/`quantization`/`numDimensions` are immutable after index creation.

Mitigating facts worth recording: "Text sent to Automated Embedding service is used only to generate embeddings and is not stored or used for model training," and "All communication with the Automated Embedding service occurs over encrypted connections." MongoDB and Voyage AI Innovations self-certify under the **EU-US Data Privacy Framework**, so the transfer has a lawful mechanism — it is a *residency* failure, not automatically a *legality* failure. But it breaks a strict "no EU data leaves the EU" posture, and it's inconsistent with your AssemblyAI-EU choice.

**Self-managed escape hatch doesn't help either:** "For self-managed deployments that are configured to use Voyage AI API key, MongoDB sends text to Voyage AI's API endpoints to generate embeddings."

### 3b. `$rerank` — confirmed disqualified, with a blunter quote than you had

From [Manage Project Settings](https://www.mongodb.com/docs/atlas/tutorial/manage-project-settings), section "Native Reranking: $rerank Aggregation Stage":
&gt; "Atlas uses Voyage AI reranking models, which incur usage-based costs. The model inference platform runs on MongoDB infrastructure in a Google Cloud cloud deployment in a US region."
&gt; "The `$rerank` stage is available only on clusters running MongoDB 8.3 or later, and it is disabled by default."

And from the [`$rerank` stage reference](https://www.mongodb.com/docs/vector-search/query/aggregation-stages/rerank/), the decisive sentence:
&gt; "`$rerank` does not run Voyage AI reranking models on your Atlas cluster resources."

Models: `rerank-2.5`, `rerank-2.5-lite`, `rerank-2`, `rerank-2-lite`. Currently **Preview** ("we don't recommend using this feature in production environments"). The standalone Embedding &amp; Reranking API (`https://ai.mongodb.com/v1/{embeddings,rerank,...}`) is the same platform.

**Good news:** it's off by default and opt-in per project. Use an Atlas Resource Policy to keep it that way.

### 4. EU regions

Vector Search and the fusion stages are **version-gated, not region-gated** — they work in any Atlas region. Dedicated Search Nodes are the only regional constraint: "available in all the regions for Google Cloud but only available in a subset of AWS and Azure regions."

German-relevant options (VERIFIED):

| Cloud | Region | Search Nodes |
|---|---|---|
| AWS | `eu-central-1` (Frankfurt) | Yes |
| AWS | `eu-west-1`, `eu-central-2`, `eu-north-1`, `eu-south-2`, `eu-west-2` | Yes |
| GCP | `europe-west3` (Frankfurt), `europe-west10` (Berlin) | Yes (all GCP regions) |
| Azure | `germanywestcentral`, `germanynorth`, `switzerlandnorth` | Subset; `italynorth` is low-CPU Search Nodes only |

autoEmbed cluster support (M0/Flex/M10+, with mandatory storage + tier autoscaling on M10+) is irrelevant — it's the inference platform, not the cluster, that's US.

`numDimensions` max is **8192**. Quantization: `none` | `scalar` (1/3.75 RAM) | `binary` (1/24 RAM, requires dims to be a multiple of 8).

### 5. BYOK / private endpoints / Data Federation — do not change the answer

Customer-managed keys (AWS KMS / Azure Key Vault, optionally over private endpoints) and PrivateLink control encryption and network path for *cluster* data. They do not touch the model inference plane, which is a separate MongoDB-operated multi-tenant service.

What *is* useful: **Atlas Resource Policies** (Cedar) can hard-enforce EU-only cluster placement:
```
forbid(principal, action == ResourcePolicy::Action::"cluster.modify", resource)
unless { [ResourcePolicy::Region::"aws:eu-central-1"].containsAll(context.cluster.regions) };
```
Pair that with leaving Native Reranking disabled and never using `type: "autoEmbed"`.

## D) MongoDB Atlas Vector Search — your 4096 assumption is stale

**The max is 8192 dimensions, not 4096.** VERIFIED: *"You must specify a value less than or equal to `8192`"* — https://www.mongodb.com/docs/vector-search/index/vector-search-type.md. Raised in the 30 March 2025 release. (Note the docs moved: `/docs/atlas/atlas-vector-search/*` now 404s; canonical is `/docs/vector-search/*`, with clean markdown at `&lt;url&gt;.md`.)

- **Every model above fits**, including harrier-27b at 5376 and Qwen3-8B/Nemotron-8B at 4096. The 8192 ceiling applies identically to `none`, `scalar`, and `binary` quantization.
- **Quantization:** `"quantization": "none" | "scalar" | "binary"`. RAM reduction **3.75x scalar / 24x binary** (vectors shrink 4x/32x; the HNSW graph doesn't). Binary requires `numDimensions` be a **multiple of 8** and uses rescoring against full-fidelity floats kept on disk. Storage:RAM ≈ 4:1 scalar, 24:1 binary.
- **Dimension guidance:** *"We recommend over 1024 dimensions when running larger workloads with quantization."* At 5.5M docs, 256d never exceeds 70% recall and 512d never exceeds 80%. So **don't MRL-truncate below 1024** — which rules out truncating Nemotron-1B to 512, and makes 2048d (Nemotron-1B native) or 2560d (Qwen3-4B) the sweet spot.
- **Data types:** BSON `double`, or `binData` subtype `float32`/`int8`/`int1`. Pre-converting to `binData float32` cuts `mongod` disk ~66%. `int1` is euclidean-only.
- **Other limits:** `numCandidates` ≤ 10000 (recommend ≥20x `limit`); 2500 search indexes/cluster hard cap; 2.1B index objects per shard; `$vectorSearch` must be the first pipeline stage; `indexingMethod: "flat"` added in preview 2026-04-07.
- **Hybrid search:** **`$rankFusion`** (RRF) requires MongoDB **8.0+**; **`$scoreFusion`** requires **8.3+**; a native **`$rerank`** stage landed in Preview 2026-06-30 but only wraps **Voyage AI** rerankers, requires 8.3+, caps at `numDocsToRerank ≤ 1000`, runs off-cluster and bills separately — so it does not fit a self-hosted German model. Use `$rankFusion` + your own reranker.
- **EU:** Search Nodes are supported on AWS `eu-central-1` (Frankfurt), `eu-west-1`, `eu-north-1`, `eu-central-2`, `eu-west-2`, `eu-south-2` (**not** Paris or Milan), and on Azure `germanywestcentral` (**not** `germanynorth`). GCP: all regions. **There is no EU-sovereign Atlas offering** — only data residency + Atlas Resource Policies. MongoDB Inc. is US-incorporated with a US control plane, so Frankfurt residency ≠ CLOUD Act immunity (INFERRED from third-party analysis, not a MongoDB statement).
